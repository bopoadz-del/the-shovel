# API — selecting tank, pressure-relief, and industrial integrity standards

Original summary of representative API publications using the official 2025
catalogue, public scope previews, certification guidance, and a later publication
notice checked **2026-11-06**. API is a standards family, not one building code.
This is a navigator for selected process/industrial subjects, not exhaustive API
coverage or a substitute for applicable ASME vessel/piping construction codes.
[Sources 1–7]

## Distinguish new tanks from in-service integrity

**API 650** addresses the stated welded aboveground tank construction scope:
materials, design, fabrication, erection, and examination/testing. A tank designed
for approximately atmospheric service is not automatically suitable as a pressure
vessel, refrigerated tank, or another configuration outside the selected scope.
The 2025 catalogue describes the then-listed **13th edition**. A later official
notice announces the **14th edition** and a **1 March 2026** effective date for
the **API Monogram programme**; that programme date is not a universal statutory
adoption or retrofit date. Do not silently apply old-edition design detail under
a new-edition label. [Sources 1–2]

**API 653** covers inspection, evaluation, repair, alteration, and reconstruction
of qualifying in-service tanks. The official fifth-edition preview distinguishes
its in-service role from new construction and explains the relationship to API
650. Remaining thickness, corrosion/local damage, settlement, repairs, and changed
service require the relevant integrity assessment; initial construction compliance
is not indefinite proof of safe continued use. [Sources 1, 3]

**Publicly verified clause navigation:** API's March 2026 inspector body of
knowledge explicitly points to **653 Section 4** for metal-loss/fitness assessment,
**Section 6** for inspection intervals, and **Section 12** for examination. It also
points to **650 5.6.3.2** for the stated shell-thickness calculation and **8.1** for
radiography. These are provenance-labelled **exam-reference pointers**, not an
assertion that the BOK itself is a construction code or that every clause remains
identical in API 650's 14th edition. The BOK directs users to its separate
publication-effectivity sheet for the exam's precise editions. [Source 4]

## Relief-device sizing, installation, and the overall system are different

The 2025 publisher catalogue identifies: [Source 1]

- **API 520 Part I, 10th edition:** selecting/sizing pressure-relieving devices
  within its stated service scope. Its published scope starts at equipment MAWP
  **15 psig (103 kPa gauge)**; atmospheric-tank venting is not simply the same
  application with a smaller valve.
- **API 520 Part II, 7th edition:** device installation and related inlet/outlet
  arrangements. A calculated orifice alone does not resolve installation effects.
- **API 521, 7th edition:** pressure-relieving/depressuring systems and the
  relevant overpressure/relief-load scenarios. System loads and disposal/flare
  interfaces are separate from selecting a single device.
- **API 2000:** the catalogue separately locates normal/emergency venting of
  qualifying atmospheric/low-pressure tanks. First select the correct service
  standard rather than extending the API 520 scope by analogy.

Use the applicable scope, physical properties and phase behaviour, credible
contingencies, backpressure, installation losses, and disposal-system conditions.
A generic pressure value, remembered discharge coefficient, or copied valve size
is not a relief-system design basis. [Source 1, respective scope descriptions]

## Resolved coefficient example — preliminary is not certified capacity

The publicly hosted **API 520 Part I, 10th-edition** copy identifies **5.6.3.1.1**
for its critical-flow gas/vapour sizing case and **5.8.1.3** for the stated liquid
case. The effective preliminary discharge factors are respectively **0.975** and
**0.65** for the described pressure-relief-valve arrangements. Manufacturer
technical guidance independently corroborates those effective factors and the
need to verify the actual device. They are not universal two-phase factors or
substitutes for other corrections. [Sources 5–7]

**5.2.3–5.2.6**, particularly **5.2.5**, distinguish effective preliminary area/
factor pairs from actual area and certified discharge evidence for final capacity.
Do not combine an effective area with a certified coefficient, or an actual area
with an effective coefficient, as if they were one valid pair. The final assessment
also retains the actual relieving conditions and applicable corrections.
[Sources 5–6]

## Other industrial interfaces in the official catalogue

**API 510** and **570** locate in-service pressure-vessel and piping integrity;
**RP 580** addresses risk-based inspection programme elements. These are not
interchangeable certificates or universal inspection intervals. **RP 500** and
**RP 505** address Class I location-classification approaches using division and
zone systems respectively; coordinate the selected approach with the adopted
NEC, actual releases, and material properties. A classification drawing is not
a substitute for pressure containment, fire protection, or operational controls.
[Source 1, named publication entries]

## Remaining applicability and limited public detail

The invoked/adopted publication edition, its addenda/errata, actual fluid/process,
tank history/condition, applicable construction code, and acceptance record are
unspecified: **confirm against the official code**. Full 14th-edition API 650
clause continuity is not established by the reviewed public scope/notice/BOK
material: **confirm against the official code** before asserting that older tank
clauses are unchanged. The sourced scope threshold and numbered
navigation above are not placeholders, but no design formula or fixed inspection
interval is invented for an unspecified asset.
