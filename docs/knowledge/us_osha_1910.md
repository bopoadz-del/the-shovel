# OSHA 29 CFR 1910 — operating-workplace safety and construction interfaces

Original summary of publicly accessible federal requirements checked
**2026-11-06**. OSHA regulations are a legal workplace framework, not a substitute
for building design codes. The regulation pages identify the e-CFR as their source;
check subsequent amendments and the applicable federal or approved state-plan
jurisdiction. No universal edition-adoption date is asserted. [Sources 1–5]

## Decide the applicable work standard, not just the building's label

**1910.5** addresses applicability of standards; **1910.12** addresses construction
work and the Part 1926 interface. A facility can have operating and construction
activities: the nature of the work matters. Do not import the construction fall
threshold as a universal general-industry rule, or assume an occupied factory
makes all work automatically a 1910-only activity. [Source 1]

## Publicly resolved fall-system examples

- **1910.28(b)(1)(i):** the general unprotected-side/edge duty applies at
  **4 ft (1.2 m)** or more above a lower level, subject to the section's specific
  provisions. **1910.28(b)(6)** also treats dangerous-equipment exposure below
  that height; four feet is not a universal permission to leave hazards unguarded.
  [Source 2]
- **1910.29(b)(1):** the usual guardrail top-edge basis is **42 ± 3 in
  (107 ± 8 cm)**. The provision permits a top edge above **45 in** if the other
  criteria are met. Height alone does not resolve openings, strength, or suitability.
  [Source 3]
- **1910.29(b)(3)–(4):** the top-rail check includes **200 lb (890 N)** applied
  near the top, with the specified downward-load deflection limit of **39 in
  (99 cm)**. This is a workplace guardrail-system check, not an automatic
  substitute for all architectural guard load combinations. [Source 3]

## Permits and energy control need real arrangements

A confined-space entry plan needs the applicable atmospheric/hazard evaluation,
isolation, attendants/communications, monitoring, and rescue arrangements under
**1910.146**. Listing a public emergency telephone number alone does not prove
that the required rescue capability and response have been evaluated. Covered
servicing under **1910.147** needs control of all relevant energy, including
stored energy and verification, rather than only switching off a motor.
[Sources 4–5]

## Remaining applicability

The jurisdiction/state plan, activity classification, actual hazards, applicable
exceptions, chemical/process thresholds, exposure evidence, and competent safety
programme are unspecified: **confirm against the official code** and current
regulations. Public rules resolve the cited fall examples. No generic exposure
limit, confined-space atmosphere acceptance, or PSM applicability is invented
without the necessary substance/process facts.
