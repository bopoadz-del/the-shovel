# ASCE 7 — loading, hazard inputs, and compatible design combinations

Original summary of **ASCE/SEI 7-22**, using public ASCE, government, and
engineering training sources checked **2026-11-06**. This is a selected technical
edition, not a declaration of local adoption. In particular, the 2021 IBC's
referenced ASCE edition is 7-16; adopting a later loading standard requires the
applicable legal/design-basis process. [Sources 1–4]

## Establish a complete loading basis

ASCE 7 addresses actions and their combinations, not the complete member design
rules for concrete, steel, foundations, or equipment. Determine occupancy/risk,
site conditions, structural system, and relevant hazards before choosing numerical
inputs. **Chapter 2** addresses combinations; choosing a resistance factor from
one design method and demands from another can produce an inconsistent check.
Strength design and allowable-stress design need internally compatible actions
and resistance rules. [Sources 1, 3–4]

Verified subject entry points include **Chapters 3–4** for dead/live actions;
**5–8** for flood, tsunami, snow, and rain; **10** for atmospheric icing;
**12** for building seismic design; **13** for nonstructural components;
**15** for nonbuilding structures; **26–31** for wind; and **32** for tornadoes.
Do not confuse Chapter 13 equipment/component anchorage with the treatment of a
nonbuilding structure in Chapter 15. These are navigation pointers, not complete
applicability tests. [Sources 1–4]

## Edition-sensitive examples resolved from public evidence

**Snow and combinations:** the 7-22 changes affect both snow-hazard values and
combination factors. Public engineering training identifies the **2.3.1**
strength-combination case **1.2D + 1.6L + 0.3S** for the stated dead/floor-live/
companion-snow case. It is one case, not the complete required set or a permission
to omit other actions. Do not transplant an older snow map or companion factor
into a 7-22 calculation. [Sources 1, 4]

**Tornadoes:** the government design guide explains **Chapter 32** for relevant
Risk Category III/IV structures in the defined tornado-prone region, with its
applicability screening. A mapped speed alone does not settle the load: location,
risk, effective plan area, geometry, enclosure, and the prescribed wind/tornado
comparison matter. Tornado loading is not a universal extra percentage on ordinary
wind pressure. [Source 2]

**Hazard-tool output:** ASCE's public tool supports edition-specific hazard
reports. Record the selected edition, coordinates, risk/site assumptions, and
report provenance; outputs are not transferable to every nearby parcel or to
another edition. A seismic report still needs the proper site/system evaluation;
wind pressure still needs exposure, geometry, enclosure/internal pressure, zones,
and the relevant procedure. [Source 1]

## Review and verification boundary

Trace actions through diaphragms, collectors, members, connections, and foundations;
coordinate components and equipment rather than checking only the main frame.
Distinguish structural strength from drift, deflection, stability, and service
requirements. No single national wind speed, seismic coefficient, snow pressure,
or soil value is invented for an unspecified site.

The governing adoption, site data, risk/system classification, and acceptance of
an alternative design basis remain unspecified: **confirm against the official code**. The reviewed public sources establish the cited edition-sensitive facts;
this summary is not the complete load-combination or coefficient tables.
