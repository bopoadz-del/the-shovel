# NFPA 101 — occupancy-specific life safety and escape

Original summary for **NFPA 101, 2024 edition**, using the public edition contents
and door-hardware industry guidance checked **2026-11-06**. This selected edition
is not a claim of universal state, local, or federal-program adoption. A contents
page supports chapter navigation, not every condition of the code. [Sources 1–3]

## Use the occupancy chapter together with the common provisions

**Chapters 4–6** locate the general framework, performance option, and occupancy/
content-hazard classification. **Chapter 7** addresses escape; **8–10** address
fire protection features, equipment/services, and finishes/contents. **Chapter 11**
addresses special/high-rise arrangements. Do not decide compliance from a door
width alone while omitting classification, protection, or the required route.
[Source 1]

The 2024 organization deliberately distinguishes many new and existing occupancy
chapters: assembly **12/13**, education **14/15**, health care **18/19**, hotels
**28/29**, apartments **30/31**, and business **38/39**, for example. Industrial
and storage subjects sit in **40** and **42**; **43** addresses rehabilitation.
Select the correct occupancy/work path rather than assuming every existing
building follows either a blanket exemption or every new-building detail.
[Source 1]

## Door/security examples resolved from public 2024 guidance

BHMA's edition-specific guidance identifies these provisions: [Source 2]

- **7.2.1.2.3.2, item 7:** the ordinary paired-door arrangement generally needs
  one leaf providing **32 in clear opening**. The 2024 exception described there
  concerns qualifying power-operated pairs whose leaves operate together; it is
  not permission to narrow every manual exit door or ignore other clearances.
- **7.2.1.5.3:** the releasing-device usability treatment is clarified for the
  egress side. Security of entry is not permission to obstruct escape.
- **7.2.1.5.3.4:** the described individual living-unit/guest-room case allows
  up to **two releasing motions**, with the 2024 clarification that the motions
  are not simultaneous. This is not the rule for every public exit door.

Use the applicable provisions for locking arrangement, listing, power/failure
behaviour, release, accessibility, and the occupancy. A reader, controller, or lock
listing alone does not establish that the entire door arrangement is acceptable.
An exception in NFPA 101 also does not automatically waive separately applicable
ADA or adopted building-code obligations. [Source 2]

## Coordinated review method

Trace each occupant's route through access, exits, and discharge, checking capacity,
arrangement, travel, illumination/signs, hardware, and protection interfaces.
Coordinate alarms and automatic systems with their installation standards without
assuming that installing one measure automatically removes another. Keep
inspection/maintenance and changed-use conditions visible after initial approval.
[Source 1, Chapters 7–9 and the applicable occupancy chapter]

## Remaining applicability

The legally adopted edition, program-specific requirements, occupancy designation,
new/existing/rehabilitation treatment, and accepted alternative are unspecified:
**confirm against the official code** and adoption/approval record. The cited
2024 door facts are publicly sourced. No unspecified occupancy's travel distance,
exit count, or protection exception is invented from a chapter heading.
