# ADIBC — Abu Dhabi building-code scope and coordination

An independently authored summary of the **2013 Abu Dhabi International Building
Code (ADIBC)**, using the official DMT-hosted publication. Its title identifies
**2009 IBC** as the underlying model edition; it is not interchangeable with an
unamended 2009 IBC or a newer IBC. Sources checked on **2026-11-06**. Confirm the
edition, Executive Regulations, local amendments, and approval conditions currently
accepted by the relevant municipality. This note does not establish that the 2013
text is the entire current regulatory package. [Sources 1–2]

## Application and companion requirements

- **101.2; 101.2.1:** the scope includes construction and changes to buildings,
  not only new-build design. Appendices do not become applicable merely because
  they are printed with the book; adoption needs to be checked.
- **101.3; 101.4.5:** coordinate ADIBC with the UAE fire/life-safety code and
  approved fire-authority updates. The 2013 text directs attention to the more
  demanding requirement when those fire provisions differ. It does not authorize
  a designer to discard a local fire approval condition.
- **101.4.1–101.4.6:** the building book relies on companion provisions for gas,
  mechanical systems, plumbing, property maintenance, fire prevention, and energy.
  In particular, **101.4.3** names Abu Dhabi plumbing/water frameworks and treats
  IPC as supplementary; saying that unmodified IPC alone governs Abu Dhabi
  plumbing would omit this local treatment.
- **101.4.6:** the energy-code obligations sit alongside Estidama rather than
  being replaced by a Pearl rating. Building safety, energy compliance, and
  sustainability assessment need coordinated but distinct evidence.
- **102.1–102.4:** identify specific versus general provisions, applicable laws,
  and the local treatment of referenced standards. An alternative international
  standard requires the stated approval/equivalence process; its use is not
  permission to mix favourable clauses from several competing standards.
- **104.11:** alternatives require authority acceptance and substantiation of
  suitability. A supplier's assertion of equivalence does not itself approve a
  material, system, or construction method. [Source 1]

## Approval and inspection evidence

**105.1 and Section 107:** distinguish the permission to undertake the work from
its submitted design information. Changes to the proposed work may also affect
related approvals; use the current municipal procedure to determine resubmission
requirements. **Section 110** addresses construction inspections; **111.1**
addresses use/occupancy authorization. A drawing review is not an inspection or
an occupancy certificate. [Source 1]

For current Abu Dhabi occupancy administration, DMT's **Administrative Decision
1/2024, Articles 1–2**, separately describes completion-linked initial occupancy,
other occupancy certificates, and phased application. This later administrative
framework is a reason not to infer today's certificate process solely from the
2013 code. The source-verified periods and later 2026 notice are distinguished below;
category-specific application and later exemptions remain authority questions.
[Sources 3–4]

## Technical review map

| ADIBC 2013 reference | Own-word design-review focus |
|---|---|
| **302.1; Chapter 4** | Establish use/occupancy and any special-use conditions before applying general design assumptions. |
| **Chapter 5, including Section 508; Chapter 6** | Coordinate building size, mixed occupancy, and construction classification; each affects the fire/structural strategy. |
| **Chapters 7–9** | Review passive protection and finish performance alongside the applicable active fire systems and local fire-code provisions. |
| **1004.1; Chapter 10** | Link population assumptions to escape capacity, arrangement, and route conditions rather than applying a single universal exit rule. |
| **Chapter 11** | Assess accessibility as a connected route/facility problem, with all relevant local requirements checked. |
| **1604.1; Chapter 16** | Establish structural performance and load inputs with the required local design basis. |
| **1704.1** | Identify applicable special-inspection subjects, responsibilities, and records before execution; ordinary site supervision is not automatically the same service. |
| **1803.1; Chapter 18** | Base foundation decisions on the required geotechnical investigation and the relevant ground/foundation provisions. |
| **102.6; Chapter 34** | Examine the treatment of existing structures and alterations instead of assuming either blanket exemption or wholesale new-building compliance. |

The table identifies subjects and verified entry points. It does not resolve all
exceptions, tables, specialist systems, or later amendments. [Source 1]

## Coordination approach

Record the municipality/jurisdiction, use and work category, local code editions,
companion standards, authority interpretations, design submissions, inspection
scope, and closeout obligations. Keep any comparable-standard approval tied to
the whole design basis. This is practical review guidance, not a prescribed
project-specific checklist. [Source 1, 101–107; Sources 2–3]

## Directly sourced 2013 numerical examples

These are provisions in the DMT-published Abu Dhabi text, not borrowed values
from a newer unamended IBC. They do not establish that later UAE fire or local
accessibility requirements are less demanding. [Source 1]

- **1008.1.1:** the ordinary egress-door minimum clear opening is **813 mm**,
  with a swinging door measured at **90°**. Occupant capacity can require more;
  **Group I-2 bed movement** has a **1,054 mm** provision, and the section lists
  other specific exceptions. Door-leaf nominal size is not the clear opening.
- **1009.1 and 1005.1:** ordinary stair width is at least **1,118 mm**, also
  subject to capacity. The under-**50-occupant** exception permits **914 mm**;
  accessible egress, spiral and assembly aisle stairs have their own treatment.
  Do not use the exception without checking its actual scope and other governing
  fire/accessibility provisions.
- **1809.4:** the shallow-footing dimensional floor is **305 mm** for both width
  and depth below undisturbed ground. These minima do not establish a suitable
  founding stratum, allowable bearing pressure, settlement performance, or the
  dimensions required by the actual loading and investigation.

## Sourced occupancy periods, not a universal certificate duration

**Administrative Decision 1/2024, Article 1(1–4)**, distinguishes initial
occupancy (**10 years**, reducible by building/use), ordinary occupancy
(**5 years**, also reducible), and temporary part-building occupancy with a
municipally set period and the stated **two-year extendable** limit or earlier
full-completion endpoint. **Article 2** separately controls conditional and
phased cases. Do not give every certificate the initial ten-year duration or
treat a permitted extension as automatic. [Source 3]

The authority's **5 August 2026 occupancy announcement** adds later application,
renewal and compliance information. Its advertised September 2026 deadline has
already passed as of this review; the [NOC/workflow note](uae_authority_noc_workflow.md)
records that update and its qualified additional-time route. Case-specific
validity and later instructions still need confirmation. [Source 4]

## Details not established here

**confirm against the official code:** the complete current amendment package;
local administrative scope; occupancy-specific heights/areas and separation
ratings; structural hazard maps and design factors; other escape and accessibility
dimensions; material capacities; other foundation criteria; special-inspection extent;
existing-building upgrade triggers; and current certificate/permit conditions.
Only the explicitly dated examples above establish numerical facts; they are not
a complete current Abu Dhabi design-value or approval schedule.
