# Site Supervision Proposal — Structure and Content Guide

How a supervision consultant's technical proposal for a site-supervision
tender is structured. Distilled from real competing submissions for the same
GCC supervision tender (civil and infrastructure construction, 2025): a
177-page and a 279-page technical proposal plus two part-numbered
submissions, cross-checked so only the elements common to serious
submissions are stated as the standard.

## Standard contents

1. **Cover letter and compliance checklist.** Addressed per the enquiry;
   confirms the offered scope, tender validity period, and enclosed
   documents. The checklist mirrors the tender's stated required contents
   item by item so completeness is verifiable at intake.
2. **Company profile and resources.** Firm profile and brochure, offices and
   geographic coverage, business lines, and statutory registrations:
   commercial registration, chamber of commerce, tender board registration,
   professional and trade licences. ISO certificates (9001 quality, 14001
   environment, 45001 OHS) listed with expiry dates.
3. **Financial capability.** Last three years of audited financial
   statements, credit facilities, and bank account confirmation.
4. **Relevant experience.** Comparable supervision assignments with project
   name, client, contract value, the firm's role, peak staff deployed, and
   duration. Tender evaluators weight likeness of scale and scope.
5. **Understanding of the project and scope of services.** The enquiry's
   scope restated in the consultant's own words, with interfaces,
   constraints, and exclusions identified from the tender documents.
6. **Methodology of supervision.** Three stages:
   - *Pre-construction:* document and design review, mobilization plan,
     baseline programme review.
   - *Construction:* inspection request (IR/WIR) workflow with hold and
     witness points; inspection and test plan (ITP) review; material
     submittal review; non-conformance (NCR) workflow and dispositions;
     RFI handling; progress measurement and interim payment certification;
     HSE oversight; meeting and reporting cadence (daily/weekly/monthly).
   - *Completion:* testing and commissioning witnessing, snagging and
     de-snagging, taking-over, defects liability period surveillance.
7. **Organization and staffing.** Organization chart; named key staff —
   Resident Engineer, discipline engineers (civil, MEP), QA/QC engineer,
   inspectors — with CVs; a month-by-month deployment/staffing schedule
   whose man-month totals sum correctly; site attendance basis.
8. **QA and HSE.** Quality policy, quality objectives, and control
   procedures; HSE policy, objectives, and the management system.
9. **Professional indemnity insurance.** Certificate and cover limit.
10. **Local content.** Localization/in-country-value plan and workforce
    nationalization compliance where the jurisdiction requires it (e.g.
    Omanization with ministry compliance report, ICV plan and statement).
11. **Tender addenda.** Each addendum and circular letter acknowledged by
    number.
12. **Appendices.** Registration certificates, audited financials, CVs,
    insurance and ISO certificates.

## Rules that distinguish a compliant submission

- Follow the tender's own part numbering when one is prescribed; the list
  above fills gaps rather than overriding the client's structure.
- Every company fact (registration numbers, named staff, certificates,
  financial figures) must trace to a real document; a proposal with invented
  or placeholder facts is disqualifiable.
- Supervision scope reviews and accepts or comments on design documents —
  it never "approves" them.
- A supervision proposal prices professional services (staff deployment),
  not construction works; contractor-scope items in the enquiry are flagged,
  not absorbed.
