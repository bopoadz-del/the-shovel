# ASTM — construction specifications, practices, and test evidence

Original guidance using public ASTM scope/use records checked **2026-11-06**.
ASTM is a standards family, not one building-design code. Select the exact
standard, edition, units, product/test purpose, and any invoked supplementary
requirements. A publisher's current catalogue record does not automatically
update the edition incorporated by a code or purchase specification.

## Concrete: do not confuse supply, specimen preparation, and testing

| Reviewed publisher record | Verified clauses | Own-word meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **C31/C31M-26b** | **1.1–1.5; 4.1–4.3** | Field sampling/specimen preparation and curing serve different purposes depending on standard versus field curing. Field-cured specimens are not interchangeable with acceptance specimens. |
| **C39/C39M-26** | **1.1–1.2; 5.1–5.4** | The compression method concerns cylinders/cores and qualified testing. Specimen geometry, preparation, age, and curing influence the result. |
| **C94/C94M-26d** | **1.1–1.4** | Ready-mixed-concrete supply/delivery requirements are distinct from placement, consolidation, and curing after delivery. C94 is not the laboratory specimen-making practice. |

The public C39 scope limits its method to concrete above **800 kg/m³** density;
that is a method-scope condition, not a specified structural strength. C31/C39/C94
also identify their unit-system treatment: do not freely combine separately
standardized SI and inch-pound values as though all rounded pairs were exact.
[Sources 1–3]

Strength test execution and structural acceptance are different decisions.
Use the governing design/specification acceptance criteria together with the
correct specimen practice and test method; see the existing
[ACI 318 file](aci_318_metric_design_values.md) for the sourced ACI acceptance
summary rather than duplicating it here.

## Steel: a shared yield level does not establish interchangeability

- **A615/A615M-26, 1.1–1.3:** carbon-steel reinforcing bars. The public scope
  lists grades **40/60/80/100** with respective SI designations
  **280/420/550/690**. Material availability does not establish that every grade
  is permitted in every adopted concrete system/detail.
- **A706/A706M-26a, 1.1–1.5:** low-alloy reinforcing bars with controlled
  mechanical/chemical properties; the reviewed scope includes grades
  **60/80/100**. Its weldability provisions still require a suitable welding
  procedure; the material name is not permission for uncontrolled site welding.
- **A992/A992M-22, 1.1–1.4:** rolled structural shapes, not a universal
  specification for all plate, bolts, tubing, or reinforcement. Supplementary
  purchase requirements and a suitable welding procedure remain distinct.
  [Sources 4–6]

Keep grade/heat identification, certificates, processing, welding requirements,
and any additional testing traceable through procurement and installation.
Do not infer a building's design capacity from a steel specification alone.

## Fire tests answer different questions

**E84-26a, 1.1–1.8 and 4.1–4.3**, addresses comparative surface flame spread
and smoke development. A low flame-spread index alone does not establish
noncombustibility, insulation against heat transfer, or assembly fire resistance.
**E119-26, 1.1–1.5 and 4.1–4.4**, addresses the performance of specified building
assemblies under the prescribed fire exposure. Changes from the tested assembly,
restraint, dimensions, openings, and installation need evaluation; its result is
not a universal prediction for every real fire. [Sources 7–8]

## Verification boundary

The precise edition invoked by the governing code/order and acceptance of a
changed product or tested assembly remain unspecified: **confirm against the official code** and the invoked standard. Public scope records support the
facts above; full test execution, acceptance tolerances, and every construction
ASTM are outside this selected-family summary. No unsupported pass/fail threshold
is invented from a test's title.
