# US model building codes — IBC and IRC

Original guidance using **2021 IBC and 2021 IRC** public ICC contents/reference
material and the technical sources below, checked **2026-11-06**. These are
selected reference editions, not a claim that every US jurisdiction adopts them
or that they are the newest publications. Adoption, amendments, and the approved
use/work category determine the legally applicable package. Clause locations
verified only from contents pages are identified as navigation, not a complete
statement of their exceptions. [Sources 1–5]

## IBC — establish the building strategy before sizing components

Use **101–102** to establish scope and the effect of adoption/referenced
standards. **105, 107, 110, and 111** separate permitting, design submissions,
construction inspection, and occupancy authorization. None is a substitute for
the others. The public ICC Chapter 35 sample identifies the actual referenced
standards; it does not incorporate every future edition automatically. In
particular, the 2021 model lists **ASCE 7-16, AISC 360-16, ACI 318-19, and ICC
A117.1-2017**. The separate summaries of newer ASCE/AISC editions are not a
ready-made replacement for that adopted bundle. [Sources 1–2]

Verified navigation points for a coordinated review: [Source 1]

| IBC 2021 reference | Author-recommended review focus |
|---|---|
| **302; Chapter 4; 508** | Record uses, special occupancy conditions, and the treatment of mixed occupancies before selecting protections or allowable size. |
| **503–506; 602** | Coordinate permitted building height/area and construction type; a material label alone does not establish the building classification. |
| **703; 714–716** | Coordinate rated assemblies with penetrations, joints, doors, and other openings. A tested wall is not automatically a tested service penetration. |
| **903; 907** | Establish which protection/alarm provisions apply; their installation standards do not independently decide every trigger. |
| **1004–1007; 1017** | Connect occupant load, capacity, exit count/separation, common paths, and total travel. They are different checks. |
| **Chapter 11; 1103** | Identify accessibility scoping before applying the technical accessibility standard. Federal ADA obligations remain a separate question. |
| **1604; 1612–1613; 1803** | Establish loads, hazard/site inputs, ground investigation, and the coordinated design basis. |
| **1704–1705; 1901; 2205** | Identify special-inspection obligations and concrete/steel interfaces; routine inspections are not automatically special inspections. |

**Sourced capacity example:** the base factors in **1005.3.1–1005.3.2** are
**0.3 in/occupant for stairs** and **0.2 in/occupant for other egress components**.
The reduced **0.2/0.15 in/occupant** factors have both sprinkler-throughout and
emergency voice/alarm conditions and exclude Groups H and I-2. Compare calculated
capacity with the applicable component minimum; sprinklers alone do not justify
the reduction. These facts were cross-checked against public fire-code consultant
guidance; they do not resolve every special seating/egress provision. [Sources 3–4]

## IRC — limited residential scope, not every residential building

**R101** addresses the residential scope; check eligibility rather than applying
the IRC to every apartment building. **R301** addresses design criteria and the
limits of the selected residential design approach. A prescriptive detail is not
permission to ignore the actual wind, snow, seismic, soil, geometry, or load-path
conditions. **R302–R303** locate fire-related construction and environmental
provisions. **R310–R315** distinguish emergency rescue openings, ordinary egress,
guards/window protection, sprinklers, smoke alarms, and CO alarms. A rescue
window does not replace the required exit route. [Source 5]

**Sourced stair example:** for ordinary stairs addressed by **R311.7.5.1–.2**,
the model limits risers to **7¾ in** and requires at least **10 in** tread depth;
variation within a flight is limited to **⅜ in** for the relevant measurement.
Winders/spirals and other special cases require their own provisions. The public
municipal detail and edition-specific technical guide corroborate these numbers;
they do not establish a local amendment or commercial stair rule. [Sources 6–7]

## Unresolved applicability, not invented universal limits

No project/jurisdiction was specified. Its adopted edition, amendments, occupancy,
construction type, allowable size, and approved alternatives cannot be selected
from a nationwide generic summary: **confirm against the official code** and the
adoption instrument. The sourced examples above are not marked as unsourced.
This note does not reproduce the full occupancy-dependent tables.
