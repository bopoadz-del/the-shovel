# KSA Construction Regulatory Authorities & Approvals

General-knowledge reference on the Saudi Arabian (KSA) authorities that govern
construction, and the permits, connections, and No-Objection Certificates (NOCs)
a building project must obtain across its lifecycle. This is an orientation note
for engineers; exact fees, processing times, and reference formats change over
time and are confirmed on the relevant government platform for a given project.

## Approval sequence across the build lifecycle

At a high level a KSA building project passes through these approval gates
(names and order can vary by municipality, asset type, and giga-project zone):

- Land / planning and plot subdivision approval at the municipality (Amanah).
- Building permit ("rukhsa") issued via the municipal system (Balady platform),
  which requires design submission and Saudi Building Code (SBC) compliance.
- Design and technical review against the SBC (structural, electrical,
  mechanical, energy, plumbing, fire) by licensed engineering offices.
- Utility connection approvals / NOCs: water and wastewater (NWC) and
  electricity (SEC), each with its own design coordination and metering steps.
- Fire and life-safety approval from Civil Defense (Salamah platform) — design
  approval, inspection, and the Civil Defense safety certificate.
- Environmental permitting (NCEC) where the project category requires it,
  typically secured early for larger or higher-impact works.
- Product conformity (SASO / SABER) for regulated imported construction
  materials and equipment, handled through procurement and customs.
- Completion / occupancy: certificate of completion and occupancy clearance
  from the municipality once inspections and utility/fire sign-offs are done.

"Rukhsa" (رخصة) is simply the Arabic word for licence/permit; it is used for the
building permit rather than being a separate branded system.

## MOMRAH — Ministry of Municipalities and Housing

The Ministry of Municipalities and Housing (official English name; site
momah.gov.sa) is the national authority for urban planning, municipalities, and
housing. The abbreviation "MOMRAH" derives from the Arabic name (Ministry of
Municipal and Rural Affairs and Housing); the ministry has been restructured and
renamed over time from the former MOMRA.

- Governs: municipal planning, land use and subdivision, building regulation,
  and adherence to the Saudi Building Code through permits and completion
  certificates.
- Issues (through municipalities): building permits, fencing permits,
  demolition and other municipal permits, and certificates of completion.
- Delivery arm: the municipalities / secretariats (Amanah at city/region level,
  Baladiyah at sub-municipal level) execute permitting and inspections locally.

## Municipalities (Amanah) and the Balady platform

Balady is the national municipal e-services portal operated under MOMRAH. It is
the primary channel for applying for and tracking municipal permits.

- Beneficiaries submit building-permit and fencing-permit applications
  electronically, with steps for design submission, technical review, and
  associated requirements (e.g. insurance/contractor assignment).
- Individual municipalities (e.g. the Riyadh municipality) publish their own
  construction-permit services within the Balady ecosystem.
- Site: municipal services are surfaced via momah.gov.sa and the Balady portal.

## Saudi Building Code (SBC) and SBCNC

The Saudi Building Code is the mandatory technical code for design and
construction, maintained by the Saudi Building Code National Committee (SBCNC;
sbc.gov.sa). Building-permit approval depends on demonstrating SBC compliance.
See the companion note `ksa_saudi_building_code.md` for the full series
structure.

## National Water Company (NWC) — water & wastewater

NWC is the state-owned (Public Investment Fund) water and wastewater utility for
major Saudi cities, established in 2008. It handles water distribution and
wastewater collection/treatment and customer connections. (The broader sector is
overseen by national water-sector bodies; NWC is the operating utility.)

- Governs: water supply and wastewater services in its service areas.
- Issues: water and wastewater connection approvals and service NOCs; developers
  coordinate network connection, metering, and (for larger developments)
  infrastructure design acceptance. Exact NOC naming and steps vary by service area.
- Lifecycle fit: connection design is coordinated during design/permitting; the
  physical connection and meter follow construction, ahead of occupancy.

## Electricity — SEC and the regulator (SERA)

The Saudi Electricity Company (SEC; se.com.sa) is the principal electricity
transmission and distribution utility and the point of contact for new-supply
connections. The national transmission company National Grid SA operates the
grid, and the Saudi Power Procurement Company (SPPC) is the single buyer.

- Regulator: the Saudi Electricity Regulatory Authority (SERA; sera.gov.sa) is
  the current sector regulator, covering licensing of generation, cogeneration,
  and district cooling, plus consumer protection and compliance. Lineage: it was
  previously the Electricity & Cogeneration Regulatory Authority (ECRA), then the
  Water & Electricity Regulatory Authority (WERA), and is now SERA.
- SEC issues: new electricity service connections and load approvals; the
  developer submits load requirements and coordinates substations/metering.
- Lifecycle fit: load application and connection design run alongside
  permitting; energization precedes occupancy.

## Civil Defense (DGCD) — fire & life safety via Salamah

The General Directorate of Civil Defense (998.gov.sa) enforces fire and
life-safety compliance through the Salamah digital platform.

- Governs: fire protection and life safety for buildings across their
  lifecycle — design approval, contractor licensing, system inspection, and
  annual maintenance certification.
- Issues: Civil Defense design approval and the Civil Defense safety certificate
  (licence), which most commercial, industrial, healthcare, educational,
  hospitality, and high-occupancy buildings must hold; licences are renewed
  periodically (commonly annual).
- Basis: Saudi Civil Defense regulations, SBC 801 (Fire Protection), and the
  NFPA standards that SBC 801 references.
- Lifecycle fit: fire-system design is approved on Salamah before installation;
  inspection and the certificate precede occupancy.

## Environmental — NCEC

The National Center for Environmental Compliance (NCEC), established by Council
of Ministers decision in 2019, is the environmental licensing and monitoring
body (air, water, soil quality, pollution control, and environmental impact).

- Governs: environmental monitoring, inspection, and licensing nationally.
- Issues: environmental permits/licences; the required study depends on the
  project's environmental category — lower-impact projects need an environmental
  management plan, higher-impact projects need an Environmental Impact Assessment
  (EIA), and the highest category adds a scoping report. The specific
  Category 1/2/3 thresholds and deliverables are confirmed on the NCEC
  licensing platform for a given project.
- Lifecycle fit: environmental clearance is typically obtained early, before or
  alongside major permitting for qualifying projects.

## Product conformity — SASO / SABER

The Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization (SASO) sets product
standards and technical regulations. SABER is SASO's online conformity platform
for imported and locally traded regulated products, under the SALEEM product
safety programme.

- Governs: product standards and mandatory conformity for regulated goods,
  including building materials (e.g. metals for construction, insulation and
  cladding, cement/hydraulic products, and ceramics/sanitary ware).
- Issues (via SASO-approved Conformity Assessment Bodies): a Product Certificate
  of Conformity (PCoC) and a per-shipment Shipment Certificate of Conformity
  (SCoC). SABER conformity is a legal prerequisite for customs clearance.
  Certificate validity terms (e.g. a PCoC is time-limited, on the order of a
  year) are confirmed on SABER for the specific product and body.
- Lifecycle fit: applies during procurement/import of materials and equipment.

## Special zones — RCJY and giga-projects

Some areas operate under their own authority and code framework rather than the
standard municipal route:

- The Royal Commission for Jubail and Yanbu (RCJY; rcjy.gov.sa), established
  1975, runs the industrial cities of Jubail, Yanbu, Ras Al Khair, and Jazan
  with financial/administrative autonomy. It applies its own regulations,
  including the Royal Commission Environmental Regulations (RCER), and handles
  permitting within its jurisdiction.
- Giga-projects and special economic/development zones (e.g. NEOM) generally
  administer their own codes, standards, and approval processes; requirements
  there must be taken from the specific authority governing that development
  rather than assumed from the standard national route.
