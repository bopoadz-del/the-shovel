# Design Objectives — The 8+1 Whole-Building Goals (WBDG)

Reference guidance on the design objectives a high-performance building must
balance. Source: WBDG Design Objectives. Best-practice guidance (how to think
about a design), not a regulation or binding standard — the project brief and the
client's requirements govern priorities. International practice, applies to KSA.
These objectives are interdependent: a good design integrates them rather than
optimising one in isolation. Grounds how the platform reasons about design
trade-offs and brief/BOD analysis.

## The design objectives

| Objective | What it means |
|---|---|
| Accessible | Usable by people of all abilities; accessible routes, entrances, and facilities (universal design). |
| Aesthetics | The form, materials, and image of the building and its fit with context. |
| Cost-Effective | Best value over the whole life cycle — life-cycle cost, not lowest first cost; value engineering. |
| Functional / Operational | Meets the functional programme; systems integration, flexibility, durability, operability. |
| Historic Preservation | Respects historic/existing fabric; adaptive reuse where relevant. |
| Productive | Occupant comfort, health, and productivity — thermal comfort, lighting, acoustics, indoor air quality, ergonomics. |
| Resilient | Resists and recovers from hazards and disruptions — robustness, redundancy, adaptability. |
| Secure / Safe | Physical security, life safety, fire protection, and natural-hazard mitigation. |
| Sustainable | Environmental performance — energy, water, materials, site, and indoor environmental quality (the home of LEED-type goals). |

Source: WBDG Design Objectives.

## How they work together

- The objectives are **interdependent** — decisions for one affect others (e.g. a
  sustainability move can improve productivity but change cost). A high-performance
  design evaluates and balances them, it does not maximise one alone.
- **Cost-Effective** is judged on **whole-life cost** (life-cycle cost analysis),
  not first cost — durability, energy, and maintenance count.
- **Sustainable** is where rating systems (e.g. LEED) live; WBDG frames the goal,
  the specific rating credits are a separate standard.

## How to use

- Use this to explain what each objective covers and that they must be balanced.
- The project brief / Owner's Project Requirements set the priority weighting for
  a specific project — this is the general framework.
- When analysing a brief or basis of design, check which objectives are addressed
  and flag gaps (e.g. resilience or accessibility not mentioned) rather than
  inventing requirements.
