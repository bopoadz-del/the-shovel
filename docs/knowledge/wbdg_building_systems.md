# Building Systems — The Major Systems of a Building (WBDG)

Reference guidance on the major building systems and what each does. Source: WBDG
design disciplines / building systems. Best-practice guidance, not a regulation —
the project specifications and the relevant codes/standards govern any specific
design. International practice, applies to KSA. Grounds how the platform reasons
about disciplines, coordination, and clash/interface issues.

## The major systems

| System | Function | Key interfaces |
|---|---|---|
| Building envelope | Separates interior from exterior; controls heat, air, moisture, water | Structure (support), HVAC (loads) |
| Structural | Carries and transfers loads to the ground; foundations, frame, floors, roof | Envelope, MEP penetrations |
| HVAC / mechanical | Heating, ventilation, air conditioning — comfort and indoor air quality | Electrical (power), envelope (loads), controls |
| Electrical | Power distribution, lighting, low-voltage/data | Every powered system |
| Plumbing | Potable water supply, sanitary drainage, storm drainage | Structure (routing), fire protection |
| Fire protection | Detection, alarm, suppression (sprinklers), and life-safety egress | Structure, electrical, HVAC (smoke control) |
| Vertical transportation | Elevators, escalators, lifts | Structure (shafts), electrical |
| Controls / BMS | Building management system integrating HVAC, lighting, energy, safety | All monitored systems |

Source: WBDG design disciplines / building systems.

## Integration and coordination

- The systems are **interdependent** — an envelope decision changes HVAC loads; a
  structural change moves MEP routing. Design is an **integrated** process, and
  most construction clashes are interface problems between these systems.
- The **building envelope** is the primary control layer for energy and moisture;
  it is the single biggest lever on operational cost and comfort.
- **Fire protection and life safety** cut across structure, electrical, HVAC
  (smoke control), and egress — treat them as a system, not one trade.

## How to use

- Use this to explain what each system does and where systems interface (the usual
  source of coordination/clash issues — see the BIM reference note on clash
  detection).
- The project specifications and the governing code/standard are authoritative for
  a specific design — this is the general framework.
