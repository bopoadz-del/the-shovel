# Saudi Building Code (SBC) — Structure & Application

General-knowledge reference on the Saudi Building Code (SBC): what it is, how the
series is organized, who maintains it, and how it is applied in the design and
approval of construction in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. Titles below are drawn
from published SBC references.

## What the SBC is

The Saudi Building Code is the Kingdom's mandatory technical code governing the
design, construction, alteration, and use of buildings and their systems. It is
adopted so that building-permit approval and design review are measured against a
single national technical baseline covering structural safety, fire and life
safety, electrical and mechanical systems, energy, and plumbing.

- Maintained by: the Saudi Building Code National Committee (SBCNC; sbc.gov.sa).
- Basis: the SBC is developed with reference to leading international codes and
  standards (e.g. the ICC family and NFPA), adapted to Saudi conditions,
  materials, and climate.
- Editions: the code is issued in dated editions — a 2018 edition and a later
  2024 edition of the code and its requirements/commentary documents are
  published; always design to the edition in force for the jurisdiction and
  project, confirming which edition a given municipality currently enforces.

## Role in the approval lifecycle

- SBC compliance is a precondition of the municipal building permit ("rukhsa");
  design submissions to the municipality/Balady are reviewed against it.
- Licensed engineering offices are responsible for producing SBC-compliant
  design and, in many cases, supervising construction to the code.
- The fire volume (SBC 801) is the technical basis for Civil Defense (Salamah)
  fire and life-safety approval; the energy volume underpins energy compliance;
  the plumbing and structural volumes govern their respective trades.

## Series structure

The SBC is published as a family of numbered volumes. The commonly cited
structure is:

- SBC 201 — General: the general/administrative and architectural requirements
  that frame the code (published across multiple volumes).
- SBC 301 — Structural: loading and forces (loads/actions on structures).
- SBC 302 — Structural: construction, testing, and inspection requirements
  (published references cite this volume as the construction / testing and
  inspection code).
- SBC 303 — Structural: soils and foundations.
- SBC 304 — Structural: concrete structures.
- SBC 305 — Structural: masonry structures.
- SBC 306 — Structural: steel structures.
- SBC 401 — Electrical.
- SBC 501 — Mechanical (including HVAC).
- SBC 601 — Energy Conservation (general/non-residential).
- SBC 602 — Energy Conservation (residential / low-rise).
- SBC 701 — Plumbing (Saudi Plumbing Code; water supply and drainage).
- SBC 702 — Private sewage disposal / on-site sewage.
- SBC 801 — Fire Protection (published across multiple volumes).
- SBC 901 — Existing Buildings (retrofit, alteration, and renovation).
- SBC 1001 — Green Construction (sustainable/green building requirements).
- SBC 1101 / 1102 — Residential Building Code (residential design requirements,
  in multiple volumes).

Note on numbering: the 1000-series covers green construction (1001), while the
residential building code is the 1100-series (1101/1102). The single-digit-suffix
volumes (201/301/401/501/601/701/801/901) are the primary discipline codes;
the intermediate structural numbers (302-306) and the "-02" variants (602/702)
are companion/sub-volumes within a discipline.

## Structural sub-series (300s)

The structural code is split so that loading, materials, and geotechnics are
addressed in dedicated volumes: loads and forces (301), construction/inspection
(302), soils and foundations (303), then material-specific design for concrete
(304), masonry (305), and steel (306). Foundation design in the Kingdom must
account for local ground conditions (e.g. sabkha, collapsible and gypseous
soils, high water tables), which is why the geotechnical volume and the project
soil report are central to structural approval.

## How to apply it in practice

- Identify the volumes relevant to the asset (e.g. a commercial building
  typically engages 201, 301-306, 401, 501, 601, 701, and 801).
- Design each discipline to its volume and document compliance for the permit
  review; retain the relevant commentary/requirements documents for the edition
  in force.
- Coordinate SBC 801 fire design with the Civil Defense/Salamah submission, and
  SBC 601/602 energy compliance with the energy requirements of the permit.
- For existing-building works, use SBC 901; for residential, use the 1100-series;
  for green/sustainability targets, use SBC 1001.
