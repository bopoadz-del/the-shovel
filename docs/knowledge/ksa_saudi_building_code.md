# Saudi Building Code (SBC) — Structure & Application

General-knowledge reference on the Saudi Building Code (SBC): what it is, how the
series is organized, who maintains it, and how it is applied in the design and
approval of construction in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. Titles below are drawn
from published SBC references.

## What the SBC is

The Saudi Building Code is the Kingdom's mandatory technical code governing the
design, construction, alteration, and use of buildings and their systems. It is
adopted so that building-permit approval and design review are measured against a
single national technical baseline covering structural safety, fire and life
safety, electrical and mechanical systems, energy, and plumbing.

- Maintained by: the Saudi Building Code National Committee (SBCNC; sbc.gov.sa).
- Basis: the SBC is developed with reference to leading international codes and
  standards (e.g. the ICC family and NFPA), adapted to Saudi conditions,
  materials, and climate.
- Editions: the code is issued in dated editions — a 2018 edition and a later
  2024 edition of the code and its requirements/commentary documents are
  published; always design to the edition in force for the jurisdiction and
  project, confirming which edition a given municipality currently enforces.

## Role in the approval lifecycle

- SBC compliance is a precondition of the municipal building permit ("rukhsa");
  design submissions to the municipality/Balady are reviewed against it.
- Licensed engineering offices are responsible for producing SBC-compliant
  design and, in many cases, supervising construction to the code.
- The fire volume (SBC 801) is the technical basis for Civil Defense (Salamah)
  fire and life-safety approval; the energy volume underpins energy compliance;
  the plumbing and structural volumes govern their respective trades.

## Series structure

The SBC is published as a family of numbered volumes. The commonly cited
structure is:

- SBC 201 — General: the general/administrative and architectural requirements
  that frame the code (published across multiple volumes).
- SBC 301 — Structural: loading and forces (loads/actions on structures).
- SBC 302 — Structural: construction, testing, and inspection requirements
  (published references cite this volume as the construction / testing and
  inspection code).
- SBC 303 — Structural: soils and foundations.
- SBC 304 — Structural: concrete structures.
- SBC 305 — Structural: masonry structures.
- SBC 306 — Structural: steel structures.
- SBC 401 — Electrical.
- SBC 501 — Mechanical (including HVAC).
- SBC 601 — Energy Conservation (general/non-residential).
- SBC 602 — Energy Conservation (residential / low-rise).
- SBC 701 — Plumbing (Saudi Plumbing Code; water supply and drainage).
- SBC 702 — Private sewage disposal / on-site sewage.
- SBC 801 — Fire Protection (published across multiple volumes).
- SBC 901 — Existing Buildings (retrofit, alteration, and renovation).
- SBC 1001 — Green Construction (sustainable/green building requirements).
- SBC 1101 / 1102 — Residential Building Code (residential design requirements,
  in multiple volumes).

Note on numbering: the 1000-series covers green construction (1001), while the
residential building code is the 1100-series (1101/1102). The single-digit-suffix
volumes (201/301/401/501/601/701/801/901) are the primary discipline codes;
the intermediate structural numbers (302-306) and the "-02" variants (602/702)
are companion/sub-volumes within a discipline.

## Structural sub-series (300s)

The structural code is split so that loading, materials, and geotechnics are
addressed in dedicated volumes: loads and forces (301), construction/inspection
(302), soils and foundations (303), then material-specific design for concrete
(304), masonry (305), and steel (306). Foundation design in the Kingdom must
account for local ground conditions (e.g. sabkha, collapsible and gypseous
soils, high water tables), which is why the geotechnical volume and the project
soil report are central to structural approval.

## How to apply it in practice

- Identify the volumes relevant to the asset (e.g. a commercial building
  typically engages 201, 301-306, 401, 501, 601, 701, and 801).
- Design each discipline to its volume and document compliance for the permit
  review; retain the relevant commentary/requirements documents for the edition
  in force.
- Coordinate SBC 801 fire design with the Civil Defense/Salamah submission, and
  SBC 601/602 energy compliance with the energy requirements of the permit.
- For existing-building works, use SBC 901; for residential, use the 1100-series;
  for green/sustainability targets, use SBC 1001.

## Discipline summaries — evidence and edition limits

These are independently written orientation and design-review summaries, not a
reproduction of the codes or a complete compliance checklist. Public source
material was checked on **2026-11-06**. Each discipline below identifies its own
evidence and edition; the section numbers are not interchangeable between books.

Most references below were verified in the publisher's **2024 contents previews**.
A contents entry confirms where a subject is addressed, not its detailed rule,
exceptions, calculation method, or numerical limit. The review suggestions below
are explanatory guidance, not additional statutory requirements.

Three evidence limits are important:

- **SBC 301:** the detailed section references below are verified for **2018 only**.
  The official 2024 description verifies the current volume's subject matter,
  but the 2024 section mapping was not established.
- **SBC 601:** only **2018 chapter-level** navigation was established, using an
  ICC-published study-companion contents preview. That companion is educational,
  not the code itself. No 2024 section numbers are asserted.
- **SBC 306:** navigation comes from the official **2024 Arabic AR publication**.
  Its introduction describes AR as a partial, educational rendering rather than
  the technical design/execution reference. The chapter numbers cited here are
  AR chapter numbers; no equivalence to English subclauses is assumed.

For all disciplines, select the authority-accepted edition and check its amendments,
referenced-standard editions, applicability, and exceptions before design or
approval. CR identifies requirements; CC includes both requirements and commentary;
commentary is not an independent mandatory provision. Do not substitute an IBC,
ASCE, ACI, AISC, IEC, NFPA, or ASHRAE clause number for an SBC clause without checking
the adopted SBC text. No numerical design limits are established by this extension.

### SBC 201 — Overall building strategy and approvals (2024 references)

SBC 201 is broader than permit administration: it links the building's use,
construction form, size, fire strategy, escape arrangements, and accessibility.
It provides a common starting point for coordinating the other discipline books.
The [official overview][sbc201-page] and [2024 CR contents][sbc201-toc] support
this scope and the following section navigation.

- **Sections 105, 107, 110 and 111:** organize permit, submission, inspection,
  and occupancy-closeout questions separately. A design package and an inspection
  record address different stages of approval.
- **Section 302; Sections 503–508 and 602:** establish the intended use and
  construction classification before checking building size and mixed-use
  arrangements. A change in use can change the applicable design checks.
- **Sections 703–717:** review the continuity of the fire/smoke strategy through
  structural elements, walls, floors, service penetrations, joints, and ducts;
  assessing a wall alone does not address all its interfaces.
- **Sections 903, 905, 907 and 909:** coordinate sprinklers, standpipes, detection,
  and smoke-management questions with the fire-system design under SBC 801.
- **Sections 1004–1007 and 1017:** connect population assumptions to exit capacity,
  exit arrangement, and the length of the escape route. These checks cannot be
  reduced to a universal corridor width or travel-distance number.
- **Sections 1103–1106:** include accessible routes, entrances, and parking in the
  early layout review rather than treating accessibility as a late signage item.

**Unverified detail — confirm against the official code:** occupancy-specific
area/height limits, fire-resistance durations, sprinkler exceptions, occupant-load
factors, exit widths/count triggers, travel distances, and accessibility dimensions.

### SBC 301 — Structural actions and hazard inputs (2018 section references only)

This volume is the loading basis for material design, covering imposed actions
and environmental hazards. The [2024 overview][sbc301-page] confirms loads, wind,
earthquake, flood, and lateral-soil-pressure coverage. The section references in
the following bullets come **only from the [2018 CR contents][sbc301-toc]**;
2024 correspondence: **confirm against the official code**.

- **2018 Sections 1.3 and 1.6:** record the loading/design basis and the building's
  risk classification so that different disciplines use compatible assumptions.
- **2018 Sections 2.3 and 2.4:** distinguish strength-design combinations from
  allowable-stress combinations. A calculation should identify which design
  framework its load effects and resistance checks belong to.
- **2018 Sections 3.1–3.2 and 4.3–4.9:** consider permanent weight, ground/water
  pressure, distributed and concentrated imposed loads, barriers, impact,
  reductions, and cranes as separate load topics rather than one floor allowance.
- **2018 Sections 26.5, 26.7, 26.10 and 26.11; Chapters 27–30:** wind assessment
  involves hazard location, exposure, enclosure, and internal pressure. Whole-frame
  wind design and local cladding/component checks are separate review tasks.
- **2018 Sections 11.4–11.6; Chapter 20; Sections 12.6 and 12.12:** coordinate
  ground-motion inputs, site classification, risk/category decisions, analysis
  method, and movement checks. **Sections 13.4 and 13.6** also direct attention
  to equipment anchorage and mechanical/electrical components.
- **2018 Chapter 5 and Sections 8.2–8.5:** consider flooding and roof water loading
  alongside drainage, including ponding sensitivity rather than only dry-roof weight.

**Unverified detail — confirm against the official code:** 2024 section mapping,
load factors/combinations, imposed-load magnitudes and reduction eligibility,
wind-map speeds and pressure coefficients, seismic-map values and system factors,
drift limits, flood levels, and rainfall inputs. No location-specific hazard value
or assumed site class is supplied here.

### SBC 302 — Temporary works, concrete execution, and inspection (2024 references)

SBC 302 is the construction volume, **not** the soils/foundations volume. Its
[official description][sbc302-page] and [2024 CR contents][sbc302-toc] connect
formwork and temporary support with concrete workmanship and the competence of
inspection/testing organizations.

- **Sections 2.2–2.5; 3.2–3.9:** treat formwork and shores as an engineered load
  path. The review includes wet-concrete pressure, construction activity, wind,
  and load transfer between floors during staged construction.
- **Sections 4.3, 4.5–4.6 and 5.4:** link dimensional control, support arrangements,
  stripping/reshoring decisions, and formwork inspection. A fixed stripping age
  is not established by this summary.
- **Sections 6.4, 7.2–7.3 and 8.3:** connect concrete quality planning with mixture
  selection, batching/delivery, and placement procedures; a specified strength
  alone does not describe execution control.
- **Sections 9.3, 10.3 and 11.3:** plan curing and weather-related execution
  controls together. The hot-weather topic is particularly relevant to Gulf
  construction, but no universal temperature or curing-duration limit is asserted.
- **Sections 12.4, 13.3–13.9 and 14.2–14.8:** distinguish organizational independence,
  staff/equipment capability, test methods, records, and reporting. Inspection
  topics extend beyond concrete to ground, steel, fire protection, and masonry.

**Unverified detail — confirm against the official code:** construction-load
coefficients, form pressure equations, permitted tolerances, stripping strength,
reshoring sequence criteria, concrete temperature/curing limits, sampling frequency,
and inspection-agency qualifications and acceptance criteria.

### SBC 303 — Ground investigation and foundation behaviour (2024 references)

SBC 303 addresses the ground-to-structure interface: investigation, earthworks,
bearing behaviour, settlement, foundation selection, and below-ground moisture
control. See the [official overview][sbc303-page] and [2024 CR contents][sbc303-toc].

- **Sections 2.2–2.6:** use the investigation scope, classification, sampling,
  and report to establish the ground model; a generic soil label is not a
  substitute for a geotechnical design basis.
- **Sections 3.3–3.10:** review excavation near existing supports, slopes,
  surcharge, grading, and fill as interacting stability and earthworks topics.
- **Sections 4.2–4.4 and 6.2–6.7:** distinguish presumptive bearing information
  from calculated behaviour, and consider settlement, vibration, expansive
  ground, and nearby slopes rather than checking bearing alone.
- **Chapters 7–8; Sections 14.2–14.4:** select and assess shallow footings,
  combined/mat arrangements, or deep supports using the ground and load model.
  Concrete member detailing is a separate coordination task under SBC 304.
- **Chapters 9–11:** identify expansive, collapsible, and sabkha ground as
  distinct design topics; one generic ground-improvement recipe is insufficient.
- **Sections 13.2–13.5:** assess damp exclusion, waterproofing, groundwater
  drainage, and water-retaining structures as related but different functions.

**Unverified detail — confirm against the official code:** investigation triggers
and borehole coverage, bearing pressures, settlement limits, compaction targets,
slope limits, footing dimensions/depth, pile test criteria, groundwater design
levels, and special-soil treatment thresholds. Site parameters require evidence
from the relevant ground investigation; this summary supplies none.

### SBC 304 — Concrete strength, durability, and detailing (2024 references)

The concrete volume connects the structural system with member resistance,
service performance, material durability, reinforcement layout, and construction
information. The [official overview][sbc304-page] and [2024 CR contents][sbc304-toc]
support this navigation.

- **Sections 4.3–4.8; Chapters 5–6:** align loads, load paths, analysis,
  resistance, movement control, and exposure-related design decisions.
- **Chapters 7–13; Sections 22.5–22.6 and 24.2:** review slabs, beams, columns,
  walls, diaphragms, and foundations by member type. One-way shear, punching
  shear, and deflection are separate checks, not interchangeable calculations.
- **Chapter 17 and Sections 18.6–18.13:** include anchorage and earthquake-system
  detailing in the design review; member flexural capacity alone does not address
  these connection and deformation topics.
- **Sections 19.2–19.4 and 20.5:** distinguish material design properties from
  exposure/durability choices and reinforcement protection. Do not infer a
  concrete cover from strength grade alone.
- **Sections 21.2, 25.2–25.5 and 25.7:** identify the relevant resistance factor,
  bar spacing, hooks, development, splices, and transverse-reinforcement provisions
  before completing reinforcement details.
- **Sections 26.2–26.6; Section 26.13:** carry the design assumptions into concrete,
  reinforcement, and inspection information. Coordinate execution with SBC 302.

The separate [ACI metric reference](aci_318_metric_design_values.md) remains an
ACI-specific reference, not a verified set of SBC 304 design values. Similar
chapter numbering does not establish that equations, tables, or amendments match.

**Unverified detail — confirm against the official code:** exposure categories,
water/binder and strength limits, cover, resistance factors, shear equations,
minimum/maximum reinforcement, slab thickness/deflection criteria, development and
lap lengths, anchor capacities, and seismic confinement dimensions. No ACI table
is re-labelled here as an SBC requirement.

### SBC 305 — Masonry systems, restraint, and quality (2024 references)

Masonry provisions address both structural and nonstructural assemblies, not
only block compressive strength. See the [official overview][sbc305-page] and
[2024 CR contents][sbc305-toc].

- **Sections 1.3, 3.1–3.2 and 4.1–4.4:** connect design documentation and
  construction quality with loads, material/section properties, and connection
  to the surrounding frame.
- **Sections 5.1–5.5 and 6.1–6.2:** consider the member's structural function,
  reinforcement, metal accessories, and anchors together; a wall's stability
  also depends on its restraints and connections.
- **Sections 7.3–7.4; Chapters 8–9:** establish the element/seismic classification
  and the applicable design method. Keep allowable-stress and strength-design
  checks internally consistent.
- **Chapter 11; Sections 12.2–12.3 and 14.2–14.4:** identify AAC, veneer, and
  partitions as distinct systems. Do not assume that a veneer support detail
  or a partition rule qualifies a loadbearing wall.
- **Sections S-1.5–S-1.6, S-2.1–S-2.3 and S-3.3–S-3.7:** coordinate product
  submissions and quality planning with laying units, reinforcement/ties,
  grout placement, and field verification. Preserve the **S-** prefix when
  citing the construction-specification part.

**Unverified detail — confirm against the official code:** masonry strength
qualification, slenderness limits, allowable stresses/resistance factors,
reinforcement and tie spacing, lap/anchor lengths, grout procedures, movement-joint
criteria, and seismic system restrictions.

### SBC 306 — Steel members, stability, and connections (2024 AR navigation only)

The [official overview][sbc306-page] confirms steel design and execution coverage.
The public [2024 AR document][sbc306-ar] provides the chapter navigation below.
AR is an educational partial rendering; technical English CR/CC subclauses and
any numerical requirement: **confirm against the official code**.

- **AR Chapters 1–3:** organize the material/design basis and overall stability
  assessment before evaluating individual members. Connection assumptions and
  frame behaviour belong in the same design discussion.
- **AR Chapters 4–8:** distinguish tension, compression, bending, shear, and
  interacting actions. Buckling and restraint questions cannot be answered
  solely by choosing a steel yield grade.
- **AR Chapters 9–11:** review composite load transfer, conventional joints,
  and hollow-section connections as different design topics. Bolted, welded,
  and concrete-interface connections need details compatible with the analysis.
- **AR Chapter 12:** consider deflection, drift, vibration, thermal movement,
  and connection slip as service-performance questions distinct from resistance.
- **AR Chapters 13–14:** connect shop/erection information to fabrication,
  installation, quality control, and inspection. These activities are not
  established by a member-sizing calculation alone.

For steel seismic design, the [2024 official series catalog][sbc2024-catalog]
also lists SBC 307 separately. Confirm the adopted cross-references rather than
assuming SBC 306 alone supplies every seismic detailing requirement.

**Unverified detail — confirm against the official code:** English clause mapping,
referenced AISC edition and Saudi changes, stability-method conditions, effective
length/local-buckling rules, resistance/safety factors, bolt/weld capacities,
connection dimensions, deflection limits, and required nondestructive-test coverage.

### SBC 401 — Electrical safety and installation verification (2024 references)

The electrical volume addresses building installations and protective measures.
Its [official overview][sbc401-page] and [2024 CR contents][sbc401-toc] use a
hyphenated section scheme. Preserve that scheme; these are **not NEC article
numbers**.

- **Sections 12-2.2–12-2.8 and 31-1–31-4:** establish the supply characteristics,
  demand assumptions, installation divisions, conductor choices, and protective
  equipment together rather than choosing a cable from connected load alone.
- **Sections 41-1, 41-5.1 and 43-1:** distinguish shock protection,
  additional residual-current protection, and overcurrent protection. One device
  selection does not automatically demonstrate every protection function.
- **Sections 52-2–52-5 and 53-5–53-6:** review environmental installation conditions,
  current capacity, conductor size, voltage drop, protective coordination,
  and isolation as connected design decisions.
- **Sections 54-2–54-4:** coordinate electrodes, protective conductors, and
  bonding with the selected system-earthing arrangement; one earth-resistance
  figure does not describe the entire protective system.
- **Section 61-4.2–61-4.4:** organize inspection, testing, and initial-verification
  reporting as distinct closeout evidence.
- **Chapters 701, 710, 712 and 722:** check the additional provisions relevant
  to wet areas, medical spaces, photovoltaic installations, and vehicle charging.
  Those chapter numbers belong to SBC 401, not to other SBC books.

**Unverified detail — confirm against the official code:** demand/diversity
factors, cable capacities/derating, voltage-drop limits, RCD ratings and coverage,
disconnection times, electrode/conductor sizes, fault-level ratings, special-location
boundaries, and inspection/test acceptance limits.

### SBC 501 — Ventilation, HVAC installation, and system interfaces (2024 references)

Mechanical safety encompasses air quality, exhaust, ducts, appliances, refrigerants,
and piping, not merely cooling capacity. The [official overview][sbc501-page]
and [2024 CR contents][sbc501-toc] establish this scope.

- **Sections 306–307 and 312:** coordinate maintenance access, condensate disposal,
  and heating/cooling load calculations before fixing plant locations.
- **Sections 402–407:** distinguish natural ventilation, mechanical ventilation,
  garages, and healthcare spaces. A general outdoor-air allowance does not
  establish suitability for every occupancy.
- **Sections 506–510, 513 and 514:** coordinate kitchen capture/exhaust,
  replacement air, suppression interfaces, hazardous exhaust, smoke control,
  and heat/energy recovery as different system functions.
- **Sections 603–608:** review duct installation, insulation, filtration,
  smoke detection, penetrations, and balancing together; route changes can
  affect both system operation and fire compartmentation.
- **Sections 1103–1106, 1110–1111 and 1204–1208:** account for refrigerant-system
  classification and machinery rooms, piping tests, hydronic insulation,
  installation, and commissioning evidence.

Coordinate mechanical fire functions with SBC 201/801 and energy-performance
questions with SBC 601. Neither safety compliance nor equipment capacity alone
establishes energy compliance.

**Unverified detail — confirm against the official code:** ventilation/exhaust
rates, separation distances, service clearances, duct/insulation classes, hood
triggers, refrigerant charge limits, machinery-room safeguards, and test conditions.

### SBC 601 — Energy review for nonresidential buildings (2018 chapter pointers)

The [official 2024 overview][sbc601-page] identifies envelope, HVAC, water heating,
ventilation/indoor air quality, and lighting as energy topics. The chapter pointers
below are verified **only for 2018**, in the publisher's public
[ICC study-companion contents][sbc601-guide]. They are not verified 2024 clauses
and do not establish a compliance route or numerical target.

- **2018 Chapters 1, 2 and 4:** establish applicability and the compliance
  documentation/review framework before assembling an energy submission.
- **2018 Chapter 5:** assess the envelope as assemblies and interfaces, including
  glazing and insulation choices, rather than treating nominal insulation
  thickness as the whole thermal assessment.
- **2018 Chapters 6–8:** coordinate HVAC energy decisions, water heating, and
  ventilation/air-quality design. An energy measure should not be assumed to
  replace the mechanical-safety or ventilation review under SBC 501.
- **2018 Chapters 9–10 and 12:** include lighting, other equipment, and electrical
  power in the energy review rather than examining the building fabric alone.
- **2018 Chapters 11, 13–14 and Appendices A–B:** locate climate information,
  reference documents, assembly thermal calculations, and zoning topics.
  The guide's publication date does not change its stated 2018 code basis.

The official catalog lists residential energy provisions separately as SBC 602;
check the scope boundary for the actual building rather than assuming all housing
uses the same energy book.

**Unverified detail — confirm against the official code:** all 2024 clause
numbers, applicable compliance paths and mandatory provisions, climate-zone
assignments, U/R-values, solar-gain limits, air-leakage criteria, equipment
efficiencies, lighting-power limits, control requirements, and exemptions.

### SBC 701 — Water, sanitary drainage, and stormwater (2024 references)

The plumbing volume covers potable/nonpotable water as well as sanitary drainage,
vents, fixtures, hot water, and roof drainage. See the
[official overview][sbc701-page] and [2024 CR contents][sbc701-toc].

- **Sections 403–405 and 504:** coordinate fixture provision and accessibility,
  installation, and water-heater safety; a fixture count is not a complete
  plumbing design.
- **Sections 604, 607–608 and 610:** assess distribution sizing, hot-water
  arrangements, potable-water protection, and disinfection as separate topics.
- **Sections 704, 708–710 and 714:** link drainage layout and maintenance access
  with fixture-unit sizing and backwater risk rather than selecting a drain
  diameter from the building's floor area alone.
- **Sections 903–906, 918 and 1002–1003:** review vent routes/sizing, air-admittance
  devices, traps, and interceptors as a coordinated sanitary system. An
  air-admittance device is not assumed permissible in every location.
- **Sections 1105–1108; Chapter 13:** address roof outlets, primary/emergency
  storm drainage, and nonpotable reuse as distinct water-management functions.
- **Sections 112 and 312:** plan the relevant inspection and test evidence
  alongside installation. Obtain actual procedures/acceptance criteria from
  the adopted code rather than from these navigation pointers.

**Unverified detail — confirm against the official code:** fixture ratios,
flow/pressure limits, backflow-device selection, drainage slopes, fixture-unit
capacities, vent sizing, trap seals, rainfall intensities, stormwater capacities,
reuse-water quality, and pressure/leakage-test limits.

### SBC 801 — Fire prevention, response, and protection systems (2024 references)

SBC 801 joins building fire systems with operational fire prevention and emergency
response. Its [official overview][sbc801-page] and [2024 CR contents][sbc801-toc]
provide the references below. It coordinates with, rather than replaces, the
building configuration and passive-protection review under SBC 201.

- **Sections 105–109 and 403–407:** distinguish permits, drawings, inspection,
  maintenance, emergency plans, drills, training, and hazard communication.
  Design approval alone is not a description of ongoing fire-safety management.
- **Sections 503, 507 and 509–510:** coordinate responding-apparatus access,
  available firefighting water, equipment accessibility, and radio coverage
  with the site/building layout.
- **Sections 703–706; 903, 905, 907, 909 and 913:** connect penetrations,
  fire-stopping/openings, sprinklers, standpipes, alarms, smoke management,
  and pumps rather than assessing active and passive protection in isolation.
- **Sections 1004–1007, 1017 and 1032:** relate escape-route design to its
  continued availability and maintenance. Store layouts and operational changes
  should be included in the fire-safety review.
- **Sections 1103–1104 and 1203–1207:** identify existing-building fire safety,
  emergency supplies, photovoltaic systems, fuel cells, and battery storage as
  additional coordination topics where present.
- **Chapters 50 and 57:** locate general hazardous-material and combustible/
  flammable-liquid topics before considering storage or process arrangements.
  The relevant hazard and occupancy determine which detailed rules need review.

**Unverified detail — confirm against the official code:** sprinkler/alarm
triggers, fire-flow and pump criteria, access geometry, emergency-power duration,
egress limits, hazardous-material quantities/separations, battery-system thresholds,
and inspection/test/maintenance frequencies. Do not substitute a standalone NFPA
rule without checking the SBC reference and its adopted edition.

### SBC 901 — Assessing and changing existing buildings (2024 references)

SBC 901 distinguishes the kind of intervention and the chosen assessment route,
rather than treating every change as either exempt maintenance or a wholly new
building. Its [official overview][sbc901-page] and [2024 CR contents][sbc901-toc]
cover repairs, alterations, additions, changed use, and relocation.

- **Sections 105–106, 109–110 and 304–306:** organize approval documents,
  inspections, occupancy closeout, structural assessment, load-testing questions,
  and accessibility review around the existing conditions.
- **Chapter 4; Chapters 5–6 and 13:** distinguish repairs from the prescriptive,
  work-classification, and performance-based subjects. Select an applicable
  route from the adopted text; this summary does not establish permission
  to mix convenient provisions from different routes.
- **Sections 602–604; Chapters 7–9:** identify the alteration level before
  assessing fire, escape, structural, services, and energy implications.
  No work-area percentage threshold is established here.
- **Chapter 10, especially Sections 1004–1009:** review fire safety, escape,
  structure, and building services when the use changes; unchanged floor area
  does not by itself settle the compliance consequences.
- **Sections 1102–1104; Chapters 12 and 14:** address additions, historic assets,
  and relocation as distinct cases. Coordinate any applicable companion code
  rather than assuming the same treatment for all existing buildings.
- **Sections 1502 and 1504–1509:** include adjoining-property protection,
  escape, accessibility, and firefighting arrangements during the works,
  particularly where other parts of the building remain occupied.

**Unverified detail — confirm against the official code:** alteration-level
thresholds, upgrade triggers, allowable demand/capacity changes, assessment and
seismic-retrofit criteria, accessibility exceptions, performance scoring, and the
adoption status of appendices/companion historic-building provisions.

## Using these summaries in an answer

Identify the **SBC volume, edition, and cited section** before explaining the
subject. Where the evidence is only a chapter heading or a contents preview, say
so. For any value, exemption, approval decision, or uncited detail flagged above,
write **confirm against the official code** and request the relevant adopted
provision/input rather than supplying a remembered international-code value.
These notes do not establish compliance or authorize design, construction, or
occupancy. They contain no project-specific design assumptions.
