# BS EN / Eurocodes — basis, actions and reference map

Citation-only navigation, checked 2026-11-06 (Asia/Dubai). These are original
scope descriptions, not extracts or design instructions. Numbers identify
standards, parts, amendments or publication years only. No design values,
formulae, technical clauses or standard requirements are reproduced.

## How the references fit together

EN 1990 supplies the common design basis. EN 1991 addresses actions; the
material families EN 1992–1996 and EN 1999 address structural response. EN 1997
adds the ground-engineering interface and EN 1998 the seismic interface. The
European Commission JRC provides the family map [J1].

| Reference family | Subject and location in this knowledge set |
|---|---|
| EN 1990 / EN 1991 | Common basis and action topics; this file |
| EN 1992 / EN 1993 / EN 1994 | [Concrete, steel and composite members](eu_eurocodes_concrete_steel_composite.md) |
| EN 1995 / EN 1996 / EN 1999 | [Timber, masonry and aluminium](eu_eurocodes_timber_masonry_aluminium.md) |
| EN 1997 / EN 1998 | [Ground engineering and seismic design](eu_eurocodes_geotechnical_seismic.md) |

BS EN denotes the British implementation of an EN document; it is not a
separate universal design regime. National Annexes carry national choices.
An annex for a different country or edition is not an automatic substitute.
The JRC FAQ identifies CEN and national standards bodies as the route to
licensed documents [J4]. A British National Annex example is listed below [B3].

### Edition and generation boundaries

The JRC family pages retain a first-generation part map. The tables explicitly
labelled first-generation below and in the linked material files describe that
map, not a list of every currently applicable national edition. Years there
identify base publications, not complete amendment or corrigendum histories.

Second-generation parts are being introduced separately, and some identifiers
and scope divisions have changed [J5]. Selected publisher-verified examples
are listed separately. Do not combine the old maps and newer examples into a
purported compatible design suite. BSI's records explain UK coexistence and
the distinction between publication and applicability [B1, B2]. Country,
adopted edition, matching National Annex, amendments and authority acceptance:
**confirm against the official code** and the relevant national authority.
This is an application check, not an unsourced design value.
