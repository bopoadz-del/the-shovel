# IECC — commercial and residential energy compliance

Original summary of the **2021 International Energy Conservation Code**, based
on public ICC teaching material and authority/technical guidance, checked
**2026-11-06**. Commercial and residential provisions have separate prefixes,
scopes, and routes; they cannot be combined by choosing the easiest requirement
from each. This selected edition is not a statement of nationwide adoption.
[Sources 1–4]

## Commercial route and mandatory interfaces

**C401.2.1–C401.2.2** distinguish IECC compliance from the ASHRAE 90.1 alternative.
The IECC prescriptive route uses **C402–C406 and C408**; the performance route
uses **C407** with its stated accompanying obligations. Using an energy model
does not automatically remove required controls, documentation, or commissioning.
The referenced ASHRAE edition must come from the adopted package, not from an
assumption that any newer 90.1 can be substituted. [Source 1]

For review navigation: **C402** concerns the envelope; **C403** mechanical
systems; **C404** service-water heating; **C405** electrical/lighting provisions;
**C406** additional efficiency options; **C407** whole-building performance;
and **C408** commissioning/completion evidence. In particular, C408 is not the
additional-efficiency-options section. Retain the route's mandatory requirements
and document any permitted trade-off. **C401.3** identifies an envelope
certificate, distinct from the system records addressed under C408. [Sources 1–2]

## Air leakage — values must retain their test boundary

The publisher's public **C402.5** change paper provides detailed 2021 provisions:
[Source 2]

- **C402.5.1.2:** applicability differs by occupancy, climate, and building size.
  A testing exception is not necessarily an exemption from air-barrier evidence.
- **C402.5.2:** dwelling/sleeping unit enclosure testing uses **0.30 cfm/ft² at
  50 Pa**, with the stated unit-boundary, averaging, sampling, and retest rules.
- **C402.5.3:** the building-envelope test limit is **0.40 cfm/ft² at 75 Pa**.
  The section also permits a defined partial-test approach. Its diagnostic
  correction/report path for results above 0.40 but not above **0.60 cfm/ft²**
  is conditional; it is not a general 0.60 design limit or an automatic pass.
- **C402.5.1.3–.1.5:** material/assembly evidence and installation verification
  are distinct. An air-barrier product result does not prove whole-building
  tightness. Seals, transitions, and service penetrations matter.

Do not compare cfm/ft² enclosure leakage directly with ACH without the relevant
area, volume, and pressure basis. A 50 Pa result is not the same test as a
75 Pa result.

## Residential provisions

**R401.2** selects the route; **R402–R404** cover the core envelope, systems,
and electrical provisions; **R405/R406** address performance/ERI routes.
Public 2021 guidance distinguishes the broader leakage ceiling from the tighter
prescriptive conditions: **R402.4.1.2–R402.4.1.3** involve a **50 Pa** test,
with **5 ACH** in climate zones 0–2 and **3 ACH** in zones 3–8 for the relevant
prescriptive requirements. Alternative enclosure-area criteria and performance
routes need their own conditions; do not present 3 ACH as the universal limit
under every route. The cited state guidance also demonstrates that jurisdictions
can amend the model result. [Sources 3–4]

## What still requires a governing-code decision

The selected climate zone, route, adopted amendments, referenced standard edition,
and exception eligibility depend on the actual building and jurisdiction:
**confirm against the official code** and adoption record. Public sources resolve
the example values above; unspecified climate-dependent insulation, glazing,
equipment, and lighting schedules are not replaced with invented national values.
