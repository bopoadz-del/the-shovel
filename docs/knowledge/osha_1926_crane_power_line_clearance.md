# Crane Power-Line Clearance — Site Safety Criteria (OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart CC)

REAL, sourced minimum approach distances between cranes and energized overhead
power lines. Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1408 Table A (Subpart CC), US federal
regulation, public domain. US-reference standard: in Saudi Arabia site HSE is
governed by Saudi labour law (MHRSD), the project HSE plan, and the utility
operator (SEC), not OSHA, but the clearance principle is universal. The reg
states the distances in feet only; SI equivalents below are converted. Always
confirm the actual required clearance with the line's utility owner/operator.

## Minimum clearance to energized overhead power lines (crane operation)

| Line voltage (nominal, phase-to-phase) | Minimum clearance (SI) | Original (US) |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 50 kV | 3.05 m | 10 ft |
| Over 50 to 200 kV | 4.6 m | 15 ft |
| Over 200 to 350 kV | 6.1 m | 20 ft |
| Over 350 to 500 kV | 7.62 m | 25 ft |
| Over 500 to 750 kV | 10.67 m | 35 ft |
| Over 750 to 1,000 kV | 13.72 m | 45 ft |

Source: OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1408 Table A. For voltages over 1,000 kV, the minimum
clearance must be established by the utility owner/operator or a registered
professional engineer who is a qualified person for electrical transmission and
distribution.

## KSA applicability

OSHA 1926 is a US-reference standard, not the governing authority in Saudi
Arabia. On a KSA site the required clearance is set by the project HSE plan and
the utility owner/operator (typically the Saudi Electricity Company). Treat the
table as a reference minimum; the utility's stated distance governs.

## How to use

- Quote the SI value for site use; cite the US original and the regulation so
  the basis is traceable.
- Before any crane work near power lines, confirm the actual required clearance
  with the utility owner/operator (SEC in KSA) — their distance takes precedence.
- Where the line cannot be de-energized or cleared, a dedicated spotter,
  range-limiting device, or line-relocation may be required per the HSE plan.
