# ASHRAE 90.1 and 62.1 — energy and ventilation are coordinated, not interchangeable

Original guidance for **90.1-2022 and 62.1-2022**, using public publisher and DOE
material checked **2026-11-06**. Selected interpretations and **62.1 Addendum ab
(October 2023)** are explicitly identified below. These are reference editions,
not a claim that they are the newest releases or the editions adopted everywhere.
An approved later addendum is not automatically part of every local adoption.
[Sources 1–6]

## 90.1-2022 — whole compliance paths, not isolated efficiency claims

Coordinate envelope, HVAC, service-water, electrical/lighting, and other equipment
provisions with the chosen route. Efficiency of a selected chiller does not by
itself establish building energy compliance. The public change summary and
performance-path training identify **Section 12** as the energy-cost-budget
method and **Appendix G** as the performance-rating method; they are not the same
baseline or accounting procedure. **Section 11** in this edition concerns
additional efficiency requirements, not the older section numbering for the
energy-cost-budget method. [Sources 1–3]

DOE's public **Section 11** application guide explains the credit selection and
building/climate-based targets, alongside the relation to Section 12/Appendix G.
A credit or trade-off must be permitted by the applicable path; improved
performance in one system is not an automatic waiver of all mandatory provisions.
Avoid selecting a target or baseline from a different standard edition simply
because it produces a favourable model result. [Sources 2–3]

## 62.1-2022 — use, population, distribution, and system intake

**Section 6.2 and Table 6-1** support the ventilation-rate procedure. The
breathing-zone basis combines a people contribution with a floor-area contribution;
distribution and system calculations then matter. The publisher's **Interpretation
62.1-2022-2**, addressing **6.2.1.1.7**, clarifies that design population need not
be the egress population and need not be specified only by counting seats; the
listed defaults are permitted when no design population has been specified.
Do not manufacture an occupancy assumption or use the egress maximum without
considering the actual ventilation requirement. [Sources 4–5]

**Resolved office example:** the office-space row reproduced in official
**Addendum ab, Table 6-1**, gives **5 cfm/person plus 0.06 cfm/ft²**, with
**5 persons/1,000 ft²** as the default density. This is not the final multizone
intake or a rate applicable to every room labelled 'office building'. [Source 5]

The standard also addresses intake contamination, filtration, construction/startup,
and ongoing operation. Official interpretations refer specifically to **5.4.1**
(intake separation), **5.5** (filtration), and **7.3** (monitoring); those are
entry points, not a substitute for their complete requirements. Determine the
correct 62.1/62.2 space scope rather than treating all residential and transient
spaces alike. [Sources 4, 6]

## Demand control and CO2 — retain the addendum conditions

**Addendum ab, 6.2.6.1–6.2.6.2**, addresses demand-control sequences, permitted
occupancies, sensor requirements, and standby modes. Its **6.2.6.1.2** retains
the building/area component in occupied-mode DCV; a falling people load does not
mean every outdoor-air term can be reduced to zero. The addendum's CO2 control
concentrations are not general indoor-air-health limits. For the default office
case, Table 6-1 gives a **600 ppm difference above ambient**, not an unconditional
600 ppm room limit. The addendum's ambient/sensor/control rules still apply.
An assumed universal '1,000 ppm means compliant' rule omits these conditions.
[Source 5]

## Remaining verification

The adopted edition/addenda, climate/building type, compliance path, occupancy,
system arrangements, and any accepted alternative remain unspecified:
**confirm against the official code**. Public sources resolve the section
renumbering, office rates, population interpretation, and cited DCV facts; they
do not supply one universal energy target, ventilation rate, or CO2 limit.
