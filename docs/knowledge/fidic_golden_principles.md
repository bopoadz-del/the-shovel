# FIDIC Golden Principles Reference — the 5 GPs (First Edition, 2019)

Structural / mechanism reference for the platform's knowledge base. Summarises
the purpose and meaning of FIDIC's five Golden Principles and how to use them to
judge whether amended Particular Conditions still produce a genuine "FIDIC"
contract. NOT a reproduction of the copyrighted FIDIC text — the official wording
lives only in the published FIDIC booklet (© FIDIC 2019, all rights reserved).

Sources: FIDIC (fidic.org) "The FIDIC Golden Principles", First Edition 2019
(paraphrased, mechanism-level); published legal/engineering commentary.
Companion note: `fidic_contracts_red_white.md`.

---

## Why the Golden Principles exist

FIDIC publishes balanced standard forms (the rainbow suite — see the Red/White
note), but almost every real contract amends the **General Conditions** through
**Particular Conditions**. FIDIC observed that heavily amended, one-sided
Particular Conditions were distorting the fair risk balance to the point where a
contract still *called* "FIDIC" no longer behaved like one.

The Golden Principles (GPs) are the guard-rails. They define the essential
character that must survive amendment. The test is simple:

- A contract whose Particular Conditions **respect all five GPs** can properly be
  called a **FIDIC contract**.
- A contract that **breaches any GP** is a distortion — it may still be legally
  binding, but it is **not a true FIDIC contract**, and the balanced-risk
  reasoning that FIDIC case law and commentary rely on no longer safely applies.

They are drafting/audit principles, not contract clauses — you apply them when
**writing or reviewing Particular Conditions**, not during day-to-day
administration.

## The five Golden Principles (paraphrased)

| GP | Principle (plain-language) | What it protects against |
|---|---|---|
| **GP1** | The duties, rights, obligations, roles and responsibilities of all the Contract Participants must stay **generally as set out in the General Conditions**, and suited to the project. | Re-writing the Engineer's / Contractor's / Employer's role until the standard mechanism no longer works. |
| **GP2** | The Particular Conditions must be drafted **clearly and unambiguously**. | Vague, contradictory, or bolt-on wording that conflicts with the General Conditions and breeds disputes. |
| **GP3** | The Particular Conditions must **not change the balance of risk / reward allocation** provided for in the General Conditions. | Shifting risk wholesale onto one party (usually the Contractor) — the most common and most damaging abuse. |
| **GP4** | All **time periods** given to participants to perform their obligations must be of **reasonable duration**. | Unreasonably short notice/response windows engineered to trip a party into default or a time-bar. |
| **GP5** | Unless the **governing law** forbids it, all formal disputes must go to a **DAB / DAAB** for a provisionally binding decision **as a condition precedent to arbitration**. | Deleting the dispute board so the only route is straight to costly arbitration/litigation. |

## How to apply them (review lens for Particular Conditions)

When the platform reasons about a project's contract, the GPs are the checklist
for "is this still a fair FIDIC deal, or a distorted one?":

- **GP3 is the workhorse.** Watch for Particular Conditions that: cap or delete
  the Contractor's entitlement to EOT or cost for Employer-risk events; make the
  Contractor bear ground / unforeseeable-conditions risk that the General
  Conditions share; or convert a re-measurement Red Book into a fixed lump sum by
  the back door. Each is a GP3 red flag.
- **GP4** — flag notice periods shortened below the standard (e.g. the Cl 20
  28-day claim notice cut down), or approval/response windows so tight they are
  unrealistic. Unreasonable time-bars are a classic GP4 breach.
- **GP5** — flag any deletion of the DAAB/DAB step or wording that removes the
  provisionally-binding-decision-before-arbitration route. Common in the region
  and a direct GP5 breach.
- **GP1 / GP2** — flag amendments that hollow out the Engineer's determination
  role (Cl 3.7) or that are internally contradictory / ambiguous.

**Relevance:** in GCC / MDB-funded work, Employer-drafted Particular Conditions
routinely push against GP3, GP4 and GP5. Naming the specific GP a clause breaches
turns "this feels onerous" into a precise, defensible contractual argument.

## Interlock with the rest of the KB

- The GPs sit **above** the Red/White mechanisms in `fidic_contracts_red_white.md`:
  those describe how the General Conditions work; the GPs describe how far the
  Particular Conditions may bend them before the contract stops being FIDIC.
- A GP3 risk-shift usually shows up in the **claims (Cl 20)**, **variations
  (Cl 13)** and **payment (Cl 14)** mechanisms; a GP5 breach shows up in the
  **disputes (Cl 21 / DAAB)** mechanism.
