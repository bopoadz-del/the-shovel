# UAE authority and NOC coordination — DM, DCD, DMT, and ADCDA

Independently authored procedural guidance, checked against the public sources
below on **2026-11-06**. A **no-objection certificate (NOC)** is an authority's
clearance for its scope; it is not automatically a building permit, a completion
certificate, or permission to occupy. Procedures vary with emirate, municipality,
plot/development authority, use, and work category. This is not a single nationwide
sequence or a set of invented statutory clause numbers.

## Roles and source boundaries

| Authority | Supported coordination role | Source boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **DM — Dubai Municipality** | The reviewed municipal process coordinates permit review, structural inspections, and completion/services closeout. | Source 1 is specifically a 2020 multistorey/FAR/public-building procedure; use Source 2 and the authority for the current category. |
| **DCD — Dubai Civil Defence** | Fire/life-safety review and the relevant inspection/approval interfaces are separate from general building-design coordination. | DBC identifies DCD's fire-code role; the DM procedure includes DCD in relevant completion coordination. [Sources 1, 3] |
| **DMT — Abu Dhabi Department of Municipalities and Transport, with the relevant municipality** | Coordinates the local building framework and municipal permit/occupancy requirements. | DMT's published occupancy decision and Binaa announcement describe different administrative functions, not a universal NOC checklist. [Sources 4–5] |
| **ADCDA — Abu Dhabi Civil Defence Authority** | Its Civil Protection Sector reviews engineering/technical plans and carries out relevant prevention/safety oversight and inspections. | Official department description; current application category and channel still need confirmation. [Source 6] |

**ADCD** is a commonly used shorthand in requests; this note uses the official
**ADCDA** designation for the Abu Dhabi authority. It must not be confused with
Dubai's DCD.

## Dubai: a sourced process outline, not a promise of today's portal screens

Dubai Municipality's **version 4 procedure dated 10 May 2020** describes three
numbered steps for its stated building category: [Source 1]

1. **Step 1 — permit application and coordinated review.** The consultant
   provides the design/application information through Dubai BPS; participating
   authorities review their scopes and upload required NOCs; DM issues the
   building permit and approved drawings. This supports coordination through a
   shared process, not a requirement for every authority to review every project.
2. **Step 2 — structural inspection requests.** The consultant/contractor
   coordinates the relevant shoring/foundation and superstructure inspections.
   An approved drawing does not replace inspection of the constructed work.
3. **Step 3 — completion/final inspection and service connections.** The source
   describes coordination with the concerned authorities, including DCD. It also
   distinguishes changes/additions/fitout cases where a DCD task is generated
   only if required. Therefore do not impose the same joint inspection list on
   every scope of work.

The current DM permit guidance also separates permit, site-inspection, and
completion subjects across building categories. Exact routing, upload formats,
fees, sequencing, service connections, and which development authority acts on a
plot: **confirm against the official code** and the current service requirements.
Do not infer live portal field names from a historical flowchart. [Source 2]

## Abu Dhabi: municipal permitting, civil protection, and occupancy

DMT's **18 June 2025 Binaa announcement** describes coordinated construction
permitting with civil-defence and infrastructure/service participants. Its
initial rollout was for private villas, with expansion described as phased.
That announcement does **not** prove that every building category has migrated
or establish the current online application sequence. Confirm the relevant
municipality and current category-specific channel rather than prescribing
Binaa, an older portal, or a manual submission for every case. [Source 4]

ADCDA's official Civil Protection Sector description supports plan-review and
inspection responsibilities. Establish the fire-review scope and current
submission/inspection gates with that authority; do not import a Dubai-specific
fire concession or assume that a municipal permit closes every fire obligation.
[Source 6; Source 3, A.4 and E.10]

**DMT Administrative Decision 1/2024, Article 1**, distinguishes initial,
ordinary, conditional, and temporary occupancy documents. Completion and the
relevant testing/systems are inputs to initial occupancy, not synonyms for every
subsequent occupancy obligation. **Article 2** sets phased application and
conditions for different building categories; in its first-phase conditional
path it includes civil-defence approval and relevant safety matters. Do not
flatten those categories into a permanent rule for every existing villa.
The later August 2026 notice below resolves part of the implementation/route
question. Category-specific validity, exceptions and newer decisions still depend
on the responsible municipality. [Sources 5, 7]

Estidama design/construction assessment is an additional coordination stream.
Identify the current rating obligations and completion interfaces rather than
assuming that a design Pearl score is a municipal occupancy certificate.
[See the sourced Estidama process and applicability summary](uae_estidama_pbrs_pcrs.md).

## Generic coordination register — recommended practice, not a mandated form

Track each approval by **authority, scope, submission stage, dependency,
conditions, accepted design revision, inspection/test evidence, validity, and
closeout status**. Separate at least:

- plot/planning and development-control requirements;
- building design review/permit and the NOCs applicable to that scope;
- fire engineering/design review and relevant specialist approvals;
- construction inspections and changes requiring re-review;
- utility/service and final-completion interfaces;
- occupancy and any separately applicable ongoing safety certification.

Use the actual approval conditions to decide dependencies. Revisions to use,
access, façade, fire systems, loads, or services may affect several authorities;
record the impact and obtain the required revised approvals instead of assuming
that an earlier NOC covers changed work. This register is author guidance, not a
claim that all six items are separate applications for every project.

## Later official occupancy implementation notice — August 2026

DMT's **5 August 2026 announcement** directs occupancy applications/renewals
through the **Municipal e-Permitting System** and identifies **16 September
2026** as its advertised application deadline. That date is already past at the
**6 November 2026** review date; it must not be presented as an upcoming grace
period. The notice describes **six additional months or another UPPC-approved
period** for parties that had begun formal procedures. It is not a blanket
extension for an application that was never started. [Source 7]

The notice identifies checks/reports for structure, civil-defence/fire safety,
gas, water/plumbing and HVAC/ventilation, based on building use. It also describes
municipal inspections and the link between a valid occupancy certificate and
registration of new Tawtheeq leases. These are later implementation facts, not
a replacement for the certificate categories in Decision 1/2024. The applicable
individual deadline, accepted extension and any newer decision: **confirm against the official code** and the municipality's record. [Sources 5, 7]

## A current, category-specific master-plan submission dependency

For the **multiple-plot/building Detailed Master Plan** category, DMT's
**18 August 2026 guidance, 1.1 General Notes and 2.1**, requires traceable links
from the relevant narrative to NOCs/supporting appendices, an approved
**Transportation Master Plan (TMP)** unless DMT gives written permission to
proceed without it, and indexed responses using the authority's review-tracking
template. **D7.1.1** identifies the TMP confirmation interface. This is a sourced
category-specific dependency, not a rule that every individual building permit
needs a separate master-plan approval. The guidance's **Section 1** identifies
**TAMM** for this planning submission, not for every municipal or civil-defence
service. [Source 8]

The same guidance's **D4.1** addresses Estidama planning evidence; see the
[Estidama summary](uae_estidama_pbrs_pcrs.md). Its public submission route does
not prove that all other municipal/fire services share the same portal, forms,
fees or review sequence. [Source 8]
