## Sample 2 (tool_calls=0, chars=8446)
**Prompt:** write a method statement for placing mass concrete in a 2.5m thick raft foundation including thermal control measures

**METHOD STATEMENT – MASS CONCRETE PLACEMENT**
**2.5 m Thick Raft Foundation**

---

### 1.0 Scope
This method statement sets out the sequence, controls, and resources for placing mass concrete in a 2.5 m thick raft foundation. Because the section thickness exceeds typical structural pours, the work is classified as mass concrete and is subject to strict thermal control to limit peak core temperatures, core-to-surface differentials, and the risk of early-age thermal cracking.

---

### 2.0 Project-Specific Technical Criteria
The following thresholds and protocols, drawn from the project knowledge base, shall govern the work:

| Criterion | Requirement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Peak core temperature | ≤ **70 °C** | `thermal.core_temp_limit` |
| Core-to-surface differential | Controlled within the project-specified **`thermal.gradient_limit`** threshold (T_core − T_surface) | `thermal.gradient_limit` |
| Thermal monitoring | Thermocouple monitoring protocol enforced to ensure the **70 °C** core limit is not exceeded | `thermal.monitoring_protocol` |
| Placing-temperature decision | Use of supplementary cooling (ice, chilled water, etc.) assessed via the project **`thermal.ice_cost_coeff`** decision aid | `thermal.ice_cost_coeff` |
| Thermal equilibrium | Time to reach equilibrium calculated from the project **`thermal.equilibrium_time`** formula using half-thickness **X = 1.25 m** (i.e., 2.5 m ÷ 2) | `thermal.equilibrium_time` |
| Shrinkage/pour strip | A **1.2 m (1 200 mm)** wide shrinkage/pour strip left open at any raft-to-podium junction | `foundation.shrinkage_strip_1200mm` |
| Dewatering | Continue construction dewatering until the permanent structure’s accumulated dead load satisfies the hydrostatic uplift stability requirements per the project **`foundation.uplift_fos`** formula | `foundation.dewatering_stop_floors` |
| Grade selection | Concrete compressive strength grade selected by element function per project rule | `concrete.grade_selection` |

---

### 3.0 Applicable Standards & References
- Project design criteria and thermal-control parameters (cited above)
- ACI 207.1R – Guide to Mass Concrete
- ACI 301 – Specifications for Structural Concrete
- BS EN 206 / BS 8500 – Concrete: Specification, Performance, Production and Conformity
- CIRIA C660 – Early-age Thermal Crack Control in Concrete

---

### 4.0 Pre-Placement Activities
1. **Formation and blinding** – Verify levels, compaction, and cleanliness of the subgrade; blinding concrete to be approved.
2. **Dewatering** – Maintain active dewatering throughout the pour and until the permanent structure’s accumulated dead load meets the hydrostatic uplift stability criteria per the project `foundation.uplift_fos` formula.
3. **Reinforcement** – Fix bottom and top reinforcement mats, chairs, spacers, and couplers (if any). Ensure concrete cover complies with the durability design.
4. **Formwork & penetrations** – Erect edge shutters, kicker forms, and sump forms; seal joints to prevent grout loss.
5. **Pour-strip coordination** – Where the raft abuts a podium, programme the pour to leave the required **1.2 m** shrinkage/pour strip open until the specified closure criteria are met.
6. **Concreting plan** – Prepare a pour-specific plan indicating delivery rates, pump locations, layer thicknesses, pour sequence, and estimated duration.

---

### 5.0 Concrete Mix Design & Supply
1. **Grade selection** – The concrete grade shall be selected by element function in accordance with the project `concrete.grade_selection` rule.
2. **Low-heat mix** – Design for low heat of hydration (e.g., high fly-ash or GGBS replacement, low C₃A cement, optimised aggregate grading) to assist in keeping the peak core temperature ≤ **70 °C**.
3. **Placing temperature** – Target a low placing temperature. The decision to adopt chilled water, flaked ice, or other cooling measures shall be evaluated using the project `thermal.ice_cost_coeff` decision aid.
4. **Delivery** – Use transit mixers with revolving drums. Record slump and concrete temperature at point of discharge for every load.

---

### 6.0 Placement Methodology
1. **Layered placement** – Place concrete in horizontal layers not exceeding 500 mm thick (or as otherwise approved). Each successive layer shall be placed before the underlying layer reaches initial set to avoid cold joints.
2. **Continuous pour** – Progress systematically across the raft. Maintain a continuous supply; plan standby pump and backup plant to avoid interruptions.
3. **Compaction** – Compact each layer with high-frequency immersion vibrators. Ensure full compaction at edges, around penetrations, and in reinforcement-congested zones without causing segregation or over-vibration.
4. **Surface finish** – Strike off and level the top surface to the specified tolerance. Provide a roughened bond surface if a subsequent structural element (e.g., podium wall) will bear on the raft.

---

### 7.0 Thermal Control Measures
1. **Core temperature limit** – The peak internal (core) temperature at any location within the 2.5 m section shall not exceed **70 °C**.
2. **Gradient control** – Monitor and control the temperature differential between the concrete core and its exposed surface (T_core − T_surface) so that it remains within the project `thermal.gradient_limit` threshold.
3. **Thermocouple monitoring** – Install thermocouples (Type T or K) in a regular grid. Locate sensors at:
   - Core level (approximately mid-depth, **1.25 m** from top or bottom)
   - Near-surface level (≈ 50 mm below top face)
   - Ambient air reference points
   Record temperatures every 2–4 hours for the first 72 hours, then every 8 hours until the concrete reaches thermal equilibrium, in accordance with the project `thermal.monitoring_protocol`.
4. **Post-cooling (if required)** – If predictive calculations indicate the **70 °C** limit may be exceeded, install internal cooling-pipe circuits (e.g., 25–32 mm HDPE or steel pipes at mid-depth) and circulate chilled water. Size flow rates to limit peak core temperature.
5. **Surface insulation** – Immediately after finishing, cover the top surface and exposed edges with insulated curing blankets (e.g., 50 mm mineral-fibre or equivalent) to slow surface cooling and reduce the core-to-surface gradient.
6. **Equilibrium assessment** – Predict the time required to reach thermal equilibrium using the project `thermal.equilibrium_time` formula with a half-thickness of **1.25 m**. Do not remove thermal protection until thermocouple data confirms both the core temperature is descending and the gradient is within the permitted limit.

---

### 8.0 Curing & Protection
1. Begin curing immediately after final set. Combine water misting or ponding with the insulated curing blankets.
2. Maintain continuous curing and thermal insulation for a minimum of 7 days, or until temperature logs demonstrate compliance with the core limit and gradient threshold.
3. Remove insulation blankets gradually (staged reduction over 24–48 hours) to prevent thermal shock.

---

### 9.0 Quality Assurance & Records
1. **Concrete cubes** – Cast and cure test specimens for 7-day and 28-day compressive strength in accordance with the project testing schedule.
2. **Temperature logs** – Maintain continuous records of core, surface, and ambient temperatures against elapsed time.
3. **Hold points** – Obtain formal release:
   - Prior to commencement of the pour
   - Prior to removal of thermal insulation / curing blankets
   - Prior to backfilling or application of subsequent loads

---

### 10.0 Health, Safety & Environment
1. Deep-excavation access and edge protection to be maintained during all operations.
2. Pump line checks and pressure-release protocols before commencing concrete placement.
3. Dust suppression and noise control at batch plant and delivery routes.
4. Spill kits available for fuels and hydraulic oils associated with dewatering pumps and agitators.
5. Ergonomic controls for reinforcement fixing and thermocouple installation within the deep raft depth.

---

### 11.0 Appendices (To Be Developed by Contractor)
- **Appendix A:** Pour layout plan and thermocouple location drawings.
- **Appendix B:** Thermal-control calculation sheet (using `thermal.equilibrium_time`, `thermal.gradient_limit`, and `thermal.ice_cost_coeff` inputs).
- **Appendix C:** Inspection and Test Plan (ITP) aligned with the project `thermal.monitoring_protocol` checklist.
